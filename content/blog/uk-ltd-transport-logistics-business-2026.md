## Step 2: EORI Number — Essential for Transport Involving Import/Export

If your UK LTD transport business involves moving goods across UK borders — importing goods from EU or other countries, exporting goods to EU or other countries, freight forwarding, customs brokerage — you need an EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number.

**When an EORI is needed for transport businesses:**

| Activity | EORI needed? |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Freight forwarding** — arranging transport of goods across borders, customs documentation | **Yes** — if you are the importer/exporter of record or acting as a customs agent |
| **Importing goods by road from EU** — goods arriving from EU into UK by road, you as the importer | **Yes** — import declarations require EORI |
| **Exporting goods by road to EU** — goods leaving UK to EU, you as the exporter | **Yes** — export declarations require EORI |
| **Operating a courier/delivery business within UK only** — domestic deliveries, no cross-border | **No** — domestic-only transport does not require EORI |
| **Operating a fleet of vans for local deliveries** — within UK only | **No** — domestic only |
| **Van hire business** — renting vehicles domestically | **No** — unless you also do cross-border transport |
| **Post-Brexit UK-EU goods movement** — any transport of goods between UK and EU | **Yes** — post-Brexit, UK-EU goods movement involves customs, EORI required for declarations |

**How to get an EORI:**

1. Apply online via the UK government's EORI service — free
2. Provide your UK LTD company details, address, contact
3. Get your EORI number — usually within 24-48 hours (often faster)
4. Use your EORI for all customs declarations — imports, exports, transit

**EORI and transport:**

A freight forwarder or logistics company with a UK LTD and EORI can:
- File customs declarations for goods moving between UK and EU
- Act as a customs agent for clients (if qualified and registered)
- Transport goods across borders with proper customs documentation
- Operate cross-border logistics professionally

**For non-resident transport professionals:**

If you are a transport professional from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, etc., forming a UK LTD with an EORI lets you run a UK transport/logistics business that handles cross-border goods movement. Your UK LTD is the entity for customs, transport contracts, fleet, and operations.

## Step 3: UK Transport Licensing — What licences and permits may be needed

Transport and logistics businesses may need various licences and permits, depending on the type of transport and goods.

**UK transport licences and permits:**

| Licence/Permit | When it applies | Issuing authority |
|----------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Operator licence** | For operating goods vehicles over 3.5 tonnes gross weight for commercial freight — required for most commercial road freight operations in UK | DVSA (Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency) — Operator Licensing |
| **Standard Operator licence** | For operators with a good record, operating standard goods vehicles | DVSA |
| **Restricted Operator licence** | For operators with limited operations — e.g., own-account transport, limited commercial operations | DVSA |
| **Driving licence** | Drivers must hold appropriate driving licence — standard UK licence, or equivalent with exchange, for the vehicles they drive | DVLA (Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency) |
| **Dangerous Goods licence/training** | If transporting dangerous goods (ADR — Accord Dangereux Routier) — requires ADR training and sometimes special vehicle equipment, licensing | ADR certification bodies |
| **Tachograph** | For goods vehicles over 3.5 tonnes and passenger vehicles over 9 seats — tachograph required to record driving time, breaks, rest periods | DVLA / DVSA |
| **Operator Compliance Assurance** — OCKA | Good practice — self-assessment or third-party assessment of operator compliance with tiredness, drivers' hours, tachograph, etc. | DVSA / third-party auditors |
| **Local permits** | Some local authorities require permits for operating in certain areas, loading/unloading, parking, operating depots | Local council |
| **Waste Carrier licence** | If transporting waste — waste carrier registration or licence required | Environment Agency (England), SEPA (Scotland), NRW (Wales), DAERA (Northern Ireland) |
| **Animal transport permit** | If transporting animals — animal welfare regulations, permits for certain animal transport | APHA (Animal and Plant Health Agency) / Defra |

**For smaller transport businesses (vans, light goods vehicles under 3.5 tonnes):**

- Domestic courier, van delivery, last-mile delivery — generally no operator licence required for vehicles under 3.5 tonnes
- Drivers need standard UK driving licence (or equivalent)
- Basic compliance — insurance, road tax, MOT, driver licensing
- If expanding to heavier vehicles, operator licence may become required

**For non-resident transport professionals:**

If you are a transport business owner from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, etc., forming a UK LTD lets you run a UK transport business. If you are operating from abroad, your UK LTD is the business entity for transport contracts, and you need to ensure any UK-based drivers and operators have the appropriate licences. If you are in the UK operating vehicles, you need the appropriate driving licence and, for heavier vehicles, operator licence.

## Step 4: Fleet Setup — Vehicles, Leasing, Insurance, and Operations

A key part of a transport and logistics business is the fleet — vehicles, leasing, insurance, maintenance, operations.

**Fleet setup options:**

| Option | How it works | Considerations |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **Buying vehicles outright** | Purchase vehicles with cash or finance — ownership, equity, capital outlay | Capital cost, depreciation, maintenance, selling later |
| **Vehicle leasing** | Lease vehicles from a leasing company — monthly payments, fixed term, maintenance options | Lower upfront cost, predictable monthly costs, no ownership equity, mileage limits, condition requirements |
| **Contract hire** | Similar to leasing — long-term hire of vehicles, often with maintenance included | Predictable costs, maintenance included, no ownership |
| **Company cars / vans** | UK LTD purchases or leases vehicles for business use — tax implications (benefit-in-kind for company cars, van benefit rules) | Tax planning important — consult accountant |
| **Driver employment vs contractor** | Drivers can be employees (PAYE, employer obligations) or self-employed contractors (different tax and employment treatment). Be careful with employment status — HMRC scrutinises. | Employment status, tax, insurance, liability |

**Transport insurance — what a fleet needs:**

| Insurance type | What it covers | When it is needed |
|----------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Commercial vehicle insurance** | Insurance for business vehicles — vans, trucks, lorries, cars used for business | Essential — all commercial vehicles must be insured for business use |
| **Fleet insurance** | Insurance for a fleet of vehicles under one policy — combined policy for multiple vehicles | Efficient for fleets — one policy, multiple vehicles, often better rates |
| **Goods in transit insurance** | Insurance for goods being transported — loss, damage to cargo | Essential for freight, courier, logistics businesses carrying goods for clients |
| **Public liability** | Injury to third parties, damage to property caused by your transport operations | Essential — clients and authorities will require this |
| **Employer's liability** | Injury or illness to employees — mandatory if you employ drivers or staff | Mandatory if you employ anyone |
| **Hire and reward** | Insurance for vehicles used for hire or reward — carrying goods for payment | Essential for courier, freight, delivery businesses carrying goods for clients |
| **Breakdown cover** | Vehicle breakdown assistance, recovery | Recommended for fleet operations |
| **Trailer insurance** | If towing trailers — trailer insurance | If using trailers |

**Fleet management basics:**

- **Vehicle records** — registration, insurance, MOT, service history, maintenance records, inspections
- **Driver records** — driving licences, driving offences, training, hours (tachograph records for heavier vehicles)
- **Maintenance scheduling** — regular servicing, MOT, safety checks, preventive maintenance
- **Telematics** — vehicle tracking, driver behaviour monitoring, fuel efficiency, route planning — increasingly used in fleet management
- **Compliance** — ensuring vehicles are roadworthy, insured, taxed, MOT'd, drivers licensed, regulations followed

**For non-resident transport professionals:**

Your UK LTD sets up and manages the fleet — vehicles, leasing, insurance, maintenance, compliance. If you are operating from abroad, your UK LTD owns or leases the vehicles, hires or contracts UK-based drivers, insures the fleet, and manages operations. If you are in the UK, you operate the fleet directly under your UK LTD.

## Step 6: Freight Forwarding — Managing Cross-Border Logistics

Freight forwarding is the business of arranging transport of goods across borders — between UK and EU, UK and rest of world, EU and rest of world via UK. A freight forwarder is a logistics coordinator — managing shipments, customs, carriers, documentation.

**What a freight forwarder does:**

| Activity | What it involves |
|----------|---------------|
| **Shipment coordination** | Arranging transport — road, rail, sea, air — for goods from origin to destination |
| **Customs documentation** | Preparing and filing customs declarations — import, export, transit — using EORI |
| **Carrier liaison** | Booking transport with carriers — trucking companies, shipping lines, airlines, rail operators |
| **Documentation** | Preparing shipping documents — commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill, certificates of origin, etc. |
| **Warehousing** | Arranging storage — warehouses, distribution centres, temporary storage for goods in transit |
| **Insurance** | Arranging cargo insurance — insurance for goods in transit against loss, damage |
| **Tracking and tracing** | Tracking shipments, providing updates to clients |
| **Consolidation** | Combining multiple shipments into one — consolidation services, groupage |
| **Customs brokerage** | Advising on customs requirements, commodity codes, duties, taxes, regulations |

**Types of freight forwarding:**

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Road freight** | Trucking — goods by road between UK and EU, within UK, within EU. Most common for UK-EU trade post-Brexit. |
| **Sea freight** | Goods by sea — container ships, bulk, FCL (full container load), LCL (less than container load). UK ports — Felixstowe, Southampton, Tilbury, etc. |
| **Air freight** | Goods by air — faster, more expensive, for urgent, high-value, perishable goods. UK airports — Heathrow, Manchester, Edinburgh, etc. |
| **Rail freight** | Goods by rail — between UK and EU (Channel Tunnel), within UK. |
| **Multimodal** | Combining modes — e.g., sea + road, air + road, rail + road. |

**Freight forwarder setup:**

| Need | How UK LTD helps |
|------|------------------|
| **EORI** | UK LTD + EORI = customs declarations, cross-border goods movement |
| **Customs knowledge** | Understanding commodity codes, customs procedures, duties, taxes, regulations. Training, expert advice, customs software. |
| **Carrier relationships** | Relationships with trucking companies, shipping lines, airlines, rail operators — for booking transport |
| **Insurance** | Cargo insurance, public liability, professional indemnity (if providing brokerage advice), goods in transit |
| **Warehousing** | Access to warehouses, storage — own or third-party |
| **Technology** | Shipping management software, tracking, customs software, documentation systems |
| **Contracts** | Freight forwarding contracts with clients — service levels, fees, liability, incoterms |

**Incoterms — important for freight forwarders:**

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) define who is responsible for what in international trade — transport, insurance, customs, risk. Common incoterms:
- **EXW (Ex Works)** — seller makes goods available at their premises; buyer collects
- **FCA (Free Carrier)** — seller delivers goods to carrier nominated by buyer
- **FOB (Free on Board)** — seller delivers goods on board vessel at port of shipment (for sea freight)
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)** — seller pays cost, insurance, freight to destination port
- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** — seller delivers goods to buyer's premises, cleared for import, all costs paid by seller
- **FOB, CFR, CIF** — traditional sea freight terms

A UK LTD freight forwarder helps clients understand and manage incoterms, customs, and logistics.

**For non-resident freight forwarders:**

If you are a freight forwarder or logistics professional from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, etc., forming a UK LTD with EORI lets you run a UK freight forwarding business — coordinating UK-EU and UK-worldwide logistics, customs, transport. Your UK LTD is the freight forwarding entity — customs, carrier relationships, documentation, client contracts.
