For German founders testing international markets, the UK LTD is dramatically faster and cheaper.

## Step-by-Step: Registering a UK LTD from Germany

### Step 1: Choose Your Company Name

Check name availability on the [Companies House register](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/). Your name must end in "Limited" or "Ltd" and cannot be identical to an existing registered name.

Many German founders choose English names for international appeal, or bilingual names that work in both German and English markets.

### Step 2: Prepare Your ACSP Identity Verification

Under the **ECCTA 2026 rules**, every director and person with significant control (PSC) must undergo identity verification through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP).

German applicants need:
- **Valid German passport** (preferred) or Personalausweis
- **Meldebescheinigung** (registration certificate from Einwohnermeldeamt — dated within 3 months)
- **Biometric verification** — done remotely via webcam or smartphone

The verification process takes **2-5 minutes** and is fully digital.

### Step 3: Decide on Directors and Shareholders

A UK LTD requires:
- **At least one director** (can be the same person as the shareholder)
- **At least one shareholder** (can be the same person as the director)
- A **Person with Significant Control (PSC)** — usually the same person

German founders can be both director and sole shareholder. There is **no requirement for a UK-resident director**.

### Step 4: Choose Your Registered Office Address

Options for German founders:
- **London virtual office** — prestigious WC1, W1, or EC1 address from £99/year
- **Director's service address** — keeps your German home address off the public record
- **Physical UK office** — if you have one (rare for remote founders)

> **Important:** You cannot use a PO Box as your registered office.

### Step 5: File Your Incorporation

Your ACSP provider (that's us) files with Companies House online. Timeline:

- **Same day** for straightforward applications
- **Up to 24 hours** on busy days
- **Typically 3-6 hours**

Documents received:
- **Certificate of Incorporation** (PDF)
- **Company number**
- **Memorandum and Articles of Association**
- **Share certificates** (digital)

### Step 6: Register for Corporation Tax

Within 3 months of starting business activity, register for Corporation Tax with HMRC. Your LTD receives a **Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)** by post within 14 days.

German founders should also consider:
- **VAT registration** if taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 (2026 threshold)
- **PAYE registration** if hiring UK employees
- **Dividend strategy** — post-tax profit distributions

## Tax Considerations for German Founders

### UK Corporation Tax vs German Gewerbesteuer

A UK LTD pays Corporation Tax on profits (19-25%). A German GmbH/UG pays Körperschaftsteuer (15%) + Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% surcharge) + Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, ~14-17% depending on municipality) — typically a combined burden of **~30%**.

For many German founders with international revenue, the UK LTD structure can deliver a lower effective tax rate, especially when combined with the Germany-UK Double Taxation Agreement.

### Germany-UK Double Taxation Agreement

The [Germany-UK Double Taxation Agreement](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/EN/Standardartikel/Themen/Umsatzsteuer/Steuerzuordnungsnummern.html) prevents double taxation. Key points:

- **Dividends** paid from a UK LTD to a German resident are typically subject to a **withholding tax** (5-15% depending on the structure)
- **Business profits** are generally taxable only in the country where the company is managed and controlled
- **German founders** should consult a cross-border tax professional (Steuerberater)

### "Vorstandssitz" and Place of Management Risk

German tax law may treat a UK LTD as a German tax resident if its "place of management" (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in Germany. If all your directors make decisions from Berlin, Munich, or Hamburg, the German Finanzamt could argue the company is German for tax purposes.

**Mitigation:**
- Hold board meetings outside Germany when possible
- Document decision-making processes clearly
- Consider having at least one director based outside Germany
- Seek professional German tax advice (Steuerberater)
