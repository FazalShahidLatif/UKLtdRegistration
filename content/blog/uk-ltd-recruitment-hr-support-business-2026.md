# UK LTD for Recruitment & HR Support: Start a Recruitment Agency, HR Consulting Business, or Talent Platform in 2026

If you are a recruiter, HR consultant, talent acquisition specialist, or anyone working in recruitment and human resources — whether you are based in London or operating from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, UAE, Egypt, Philippines, Sri Lanka, or anywhere in the world — a UK LTD is the legal entity that lets you run a professional recruitment or HR business, place candidates with UK employers, offer HR consulting services, build a talent platform, and build a recruitment/HR brand under one structure.

This guide covers exactly what a UK LTD means for recruitment and HR businesses.

## Why a UK LTD for Recruitment and HR

Recruitment and HR are high-trust, relationship-driven businesses. UK employers want to work with professional, registered entities — not informal recruiters. A UK LTD gives you:

| What a non-LTD recruiter/HR professional faces | What a UK LTD recruitment/HR business gets |
|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| UK employers may not take an individual recruiter seriously | A UK LTD is a recognised business — UK employers trust a registered company for recruitment partnerships |
| Personal liability for recruitment mistakes | UK LTD = limited liability for placement disputes, compliance issues, employment law matters |
| Difficulty receiving placement fees from UK employers | UK LTD bank account in GBP receives placement fees cleanly from UK employers |
| Difficulty placing candidates internationally | UK LTD = entity that can coordinate international placements, visa support, relocation |
| No brand | UK LTD = brand — recruitment agency name, reputation, website, candidate and client relationships |
| Difficulty scaling | UK LTD = scalable — add recruiters, specialise in industries, expand to new regions, build platforms |
| No structure for HR consulting | UK LTD = consulting entity — HR audits, policy development, compliance advice, training |

**For non-resident recruiters and HR professionals**, a UK LTD is the bridge that lets you operate in UK recruitment and HR from abroad — placing candidates, building employer relationships, offering HR consulting, and building a talent brand that spans countries.

## Step 2: Recruitment Agency Business Models

Recruitment agencies operate in different ways. Your UK LTD can adopt one or more of these models.

**Recruitment agency models:**

| Model | How it works | Typical fee |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Contingency recruitment** | You recruit for a vacancy — employer pays fee only when you place a candidate | 15-25% of candidate's first annual salary (varies by industry and level) |
| **Retained search** | Employer pays you a retainer (upfront or staged) to find a candidate — you get paid whether or not you place (retainer is usually non-refundable deposit) | Retainer + success fee (often 20-30% of salary) |
| **Exclusive recruitment** | Employer gives you exclusive right to recruit for a role — only you recruit | Standard contingency or retained fee |
| **Temporary / temp staffing** | You provide temporary workers to employers for a period — you employ or contract the temp, employer pays your bill rate (pay rate + markup) | Markup of 20-50% on temp's pay rate, depending on industry and skill level |
| **Contract recruitment** | Similar to temp — contractors placed at client sites for fixed terms | Markup on contractor's rate |
| **Executive search** | Senior appointments — directors, heads of department, C-suite, partners | Higher fees — 20-35% of compensation |
| **Specialist / niche recruitment** | Specialising in one industry or function — IT, finance, healthcare, legal, construction, etc. | Standard contingency or retained, but specialist expertise commands premium |
| **RPO (Recruitment Process Outsourcing)** | You manage part or all of an employer's recruitment process — acting as their internal recruitment team | Managed service fee — monthly retainer, per-hire fee, or hybrid |

**For non-resident recruiters:**

A UK LTD can operate any of these models. The most common starting point is contingency recruitment in a specialist area — you recruit for UK employers in your area of expertise, place candidates, and earn a fee on placement.

## Step 4: Placing Candidates — How Recruitment Works

The core of recruitment is placing candidates with employers. Here is how it works.

**The recruitment process:**

1. **Vacancy received** — Employer contacts you (or you identify an opportunity)
2. **Vacancy brief** — Understand the role — job title, responsibilities, skills, experience, salary, location, start date, contract type
3. **Candidate sourcing** — Search your database, job boards, LinkedIn, referrals, direct outreach, networking, adverts
4. **Screening** — Review CVs, assess fit against the vacancy, check skills and experience, availability, salary expectations
5. **Presenting candidates** — Send CVs and candidate profiles to employer, with your recommendations
6. **Employer interviews** — Employer interviews shortlisted candidates — may be multiple rounds
7. **Offer** — Employer makes offer — salary, start date, terms
8. **Candidate acceptance** — Candidate accepts, resigns from current role (if employed)
9. **Pre-employment checks** — References, right to work, qualification verification, DBS check (if required)
10. **Placement fee** — Candidate starts, fee invoiced to employer (typically 7-30 days from start, depending on agreement)

**Fee structures in recruitment:**

| Fee type | Typical arrangement |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Percentage of salary** | 15-25% of candidate's first annual salary (permanent roles) — varies by industry, level, specialisation |
| **Markup on temp rate** | Temp bill rate = pay rate + markup — markup typically 20-50% depending on skill, industry, market |
| **Executive / senior** | Higher fees — 20-35% of compensation for senior and executive roles |
| **Junior / volume** | Lower fees — 10-15% for junior roles, high volumes |

**Guarantee period:**

Most recruitment agreements include a guarantee period — if the candidate leaves within the guarantee period (typically 3-6 months), the fee is reduced or refunded (partially or fully). This protects the employer. Recruiters should be confident in their placements to offer a guarantee.

**Tips for recruiters:**

1. **Build a candidate database** — Your candidates are your asset. Maintain a database, keep in touch, update their career progression, be ready when vacancies arise.
2. **Build employer relationships** — Know the hiring managers, HR, and recruitment contacts at your target employers. Relationships drive repeat business.
3. **Specialise** — See Step 3. Specialisation is the foundation of a successful recruitment business.
4. **Understand the market** — Salary levels, hiring trends, candidate availability, skills in demand. The more you know, the better you advise employers and candidates.
5. **Be honest and professional** — Recruitment is a relationship business. Don't oversell candidates, don't misrepresent vacancies, do deliver on promises. Reputation matters.
6. **Use multiple sourcing channels** — Database, job boards, LinkedIn, referrals, direct outreach, networking, adverts, social media. Don't rely on one channel.
7. **Follow up** — Candidates and employers both need follow-up. Keep candidates informed, keep employers updated. Good communication distinguishes good recruiters.

## Step 5: International Recruitment — UK LTD as the Bridge

This is where a UK LTD recruiter has a unique advantage — placing candidates from other countries into UK roles.

**Why international recruitment matters:**

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **UK employers need diverse talent** | UK employers increasingly seek diverse candidates — multilingual, international experience, different cultural perspectives. Candidates from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, UAE, etc. bring these qualities. |
| **Skills shortages in UK** | UK has skills shortages in many sectors — IT, healthcare, construction, engineering, hospitality, customer service. International candidates can fill these gaps. |
| **Language skills are valuable** | Candidates with Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, Arabic, French, German, Spanish, Mandarin, etc. are valuable to UK employers with international operations, diverse customer bases, global teams. |
| **Commonwealth legal and education background** | Candidates from Commonwealth countries (Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, etc.) often have legal, educational, and professional backgrounds familiar to UK employers — easier to assess and integrate. |
| **Cost-effective talent** | International candidates can be cost-effective for UK employers — while still being qualified and skilled. |

**What a UK LTD international recruiter does:**

1. **Source candidates internationally** — from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, UAE, etc. — candidates with relevant skills, experience, language, and ambition to work in UK
2. **Assess candidates** — skills, experience, English language ability, UK work eligibility (visa requirements, sponsorship), cultural fit
3. **Advise on UK work visas** — if candidates need UK sponsorship (Skilled Worker visa), advise on the process, requirements, and connect with employers who can sponsor
4. **Introduce to UK employers** — present candidates to UK employers, explain their background, skills, fit for the role
5. **Support relocation** — practical advice on moving to UK — housing, banking (Wise/Revolut for receiving international payments during transition), cost of living, settling in

**UK work visas for international candidates — basics for recruiters:**

| Visa type | Relevance for recruitment |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Skilled Worker visa** | Main route for skilled workers sponsored by a UK employer. Employer must be a licensed sponsor. Candidate must have a job offer at the required skill level and salary threshold. Relevant for many professional roles. |
| **Health and Care Worker visa** | For healthcare professionals — doctors, nurses, health professionals. Sponsored by UK health and care employers. Faster, lower fee route for eligible roles. |
| **Global Talent visa** | For leaders or potential leaders in specific fields — digital technology, arts and culture, academia, research. Not employer-sponsored — candidate applies on merit. |
| **Graduate visa** | For international students who have graduated from a UK university — can work in UK for 2-3 years without sponsorship. Relevant for candidates who studied in UK. |
| **High Potential Individual (HPI) visa** | For graduates of top global universities (list published by UK) — can work in UK for 2 years without sponsorship. |
| **Youth Mobility Scheme** | For young adults (18-30) from eligible countries — work in UK for up to 2 years. Limited to certain countries. |
| **Temporary Worker visa** | Various sub-categories — creative workers, sports people, voluntary workers, etc. |

**For recruiters placing international candidates:**

Understanding UK work visas is valuable — you can advise candidates on their options, and you can help employers understand the sponsorship process. Not all roles qualify for Skilled Worker sponsorship — the role must be at the required skill level and the salary must meet the threshold. Some roles are on the shortage occupation list — which may give lower salary thresholds or other advantages.
