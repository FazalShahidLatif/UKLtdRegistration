# UK LTD for Study Visa Consultancy: Start a UK University Agent Business, Partner with UK Universities, and Build a Consultancy in 2026

If you are a study visa consultant, education consultant, or anyone who helps students apply to UK universities — whether you are based in London or operating from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Egypt, UAE, Sri Lanka, Philippines, or anywhere in the world — a UK LTD is the legal entity that lets you run a professional education consultancy business, partner with UK universities as an official agent, advise students on UK study visas, earn commission from UK degree referrals, and build an education consultancy brand under one structure.

This guide covers exactly what a UK LTD means for study visa consultancy and education consulting businesses.

---

## Why a UK LTD for Study Visa Consultancy

The UK is one of the world's top destinations for international students — world-class universities, English-language education, post-study work opportunities, and a global reputation. A UK LTD gives education consultants:

| What a non-LTD education consultant faces | What a UK LTD education consultancy gets |
|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Universities may not take an individual consultant seriously as an agent | A UK LTD is a recognised business — UK universities take a registered company seriously as a potential agent partner |
| Personal liability for consultancy mistakes | UK LTD = limited liability — consultancy disputes, visa advice issues, compliance issues sit with the company |
| Difficulty receiving payments from students and universities | UK LTD bank account in GBP receives fees from students and commission from universities cleanly |
| Difficulty placing international students | UK LTD = entity that can coordinate student applications, visa support, accommodation, scholarship advice |
| No brand | UK LTD = brand — education consultancy name, reputation, website, student and university relationships |
| Difficulty scaling | UK LTD = scalable — add consultants, specialise in regions/levels, expand to new universities, build platforms |
| No structure for university partnerships | UK LTD = partner entity — signs agent agreements with UK universities, attends agent events, builds relationships |

**For non-resident education consultants**, a UK LTD is the bridge that lets you operate a UK education consultancy from abroad — advising students, partnering with UK universities, placing students, and building an education brand that spans countries.

## Step 3: UK University Agent Programmes — Partnering with Universities

UK universities have agent programmes — they appoint education agents in countries around the world to recruit international students. A UK LTD education consultancy can become a UK-based agent partner with UK universities.

**What a UK university agent does:**

| Role | What it involves |
|------|------------------|
| **Student recruitment** | Promoting UK universities and courses to students in your market — counselling, advising, encouraging applications. |
| **Student counselling** | Advising students on course choices, university choices, career pathways, entry requirements, application process. |
| **Application support** | Supporting students with applications — UCAS, direct applications, personal statements, references, documents. |
| **Visa guidance** | Advising students on UK study visas — requirements, CAS, financial requirements, application process. |
| **Student support** | Supporting students through the process — from initial inquiry to arrival in UK. |
| **Feedback to universities** | Providing market intelligence to universities — student trends, interests, concerns, market conditions. |
| **Promoting universities** | Attending education fairs, events, roadshows — representing universities, promoting courses. |
| **Promoting UK study** | Promoting UK as a study destination — highlighting benefits, opportunities, experiences. |

**University agent programmes — how they work:**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Agent agreement** | University signs an agreement with the agent — roles, responsibilities, commission/fees, compliance, code of conduct, territories, student recruitment targets. |
| **Commission / fees** | Universities pay agents commission for recruited students — typically a commission per recruited student, often paid after the student enrols. Commission varies — can be hundreds to thousands of GBP per student, depending on the university, course, and agreement. Some universities pay a retainer or annual fee in addition to or instead of commission. |
| **Agent certification / approval** | Universities approve agents — an agent needs to be approved by each university they work with. Some universities have formal agent certification processes. |
| **Agent networks** | Universities may be part of agent networks — some agencies work with many universities, some specialise in a few. |
| **Compliance** | Agents must comply with university agent policies, UKVI regulations (for visa advice), Student Route compliance, ethical recruitment standards (e.g., UCAS Agent Code of Practice, British Council Agent Code of Conduct). |
| **Territory** | Agents may be appointed for specific territories or countries — e.g., an agent for Pakistan, an agent for Nigeria, an agent for the Gulf. |
| **Commission payment** | Commission is usually paid to the agent after the student enrols and starts studying — sometimes after a certain period. Payment terms in the agent agreement. |

**For non-resident education consultants:**

If you are an education consultant from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, etc., forming a UK LTD gives you a UK entity to partner with UK universities as an agent. Your UK LTD is the agent entity — it signs agent agreements, receives commission, provides student support, and builds relationships with UK universities. If you are based in the UK, you can attend UK agent events, meet university accreditations teams, and build relationships in person. If you are based abroad, your UK LTD is the UK-facing entity for the partnership.

## Step 4: UK Study Visas — Advising Students on Visa Pathways

A key service for study visa consultancies is advising students on UK study visas. This is a valuable service — visa requirements can be complex, and students need clear, accurate guidance.

**UK study visa types:**

| Visa type | Who it is for | Key requirements |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|
| **Student visa** (formerly Tier 4) | International students studying in UK — degree-level and above, and some below degree level with an approved sponsor | Offered by an approved UK sponsor (university or college with Student Route sponsor licence). CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) from sponsor. Financial requirement (tuition + living costs, funds held for 28 days). English language requirement (unless exempt). |
| **Child Student visa** | Children aged 4-17 studying at independent schools in UK | Offer from independent school with Child Student sponsor licence. Parental/guardian involvement. Financial requirement. |
| **Graduate visa** (Graduate route) | International students who have completed a UK degree — can work in UK for 2 years (3 years for PhD) without sponsorship | Completed eligible UK degree. Applied from inside UK. No sponsor needed. |
| **Short-term study visa** | Short courses in UK — up to 6 months (or 11 months for English language courses) | Acceptance on an accredited course. Financial requirement. |
| **Parent of a Child Student visa** | Parents of Child Students studying at independent schools in UK | Child has Child Student visa. Financial requirement. |

**Student visa requirements — key points for advisers:**

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Approved sponsor (CAS)** | Student must have an offer from an approved UK sponsor (university or college with Student Route sponsor licence). The sponsor issues a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) — a unique reference number with details of the course, fees, etc. |
| **Financial requirement** | Student must show they have enough money to cover tuition fees (or have paid them) and living costs for up to 9 months (for courses in London or outside London). Funds must be held for 28 consecutive days before application. |
| **English language** | Student must meet English language requirement — typically IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, or other approved test, or a degree taught in English, or be from an exempt country. |
| **Course level** | Student visa is available for degree-level study and some below-degree-level study (with an approved sponsor). |
| **Work rights** | Student visa holders can work part-time during term (usually up to 20 hours per week for degree-level students) and full-time during vacations. Some courses have restrictions. |
| **Dependents** | Student visa holders on degree-level courses can bring dependent partners and children (with their own visa requirements). Dependents can work (with some restrictions) and study. |
| **Health surcharge** | Student visa holders pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) — access to NHS healthcare during stay. |
| **Visa application** | Apply online, pay visa fee, pay IHS, provide biometrics (fingerprints, photo), attend visa application centre. |

**What a study visa consultancy advises on:**

- **Which visa is right for the student** — Student visa, short-term study, etc.
- **CAS and sponsor requirements** — understanding CAS, approved sponsors, course levels.
- **Financial requirements** — how much money is needed, how to show it, 28-day rule, acceptable funds.
- **English language** — tests, scores, exemptions, alternatives.
- **Visa application process** — step by step, documents needed, application form, biometrics, timing.
- **Visa refusal and reapplication** — understanding reasons for refusal, how to address issues, reapplication advice.
- **Dependents** — bringing family, requirements, work rights for dependents.
- **Post-study options** — Graduate visa, Skilled Worker visa, job search, career pathways after studies.

**Important for consultancies:**

- Visa advice must be accurate and up-to-date. UK visa rules change. Stay informed of current requirements.
- Visa advice is not legal advice — be clear that you are advising on the process, not providing legal representation. For complex cases, refer to immigration lawyers or OISC-registered advisers (Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner).
- UKVI compliance is important — consultancies should operate ethically and accurately, not guarantee visas or make false promises.

## Step 5: Earning Commission from UK Degree Referrals

A key revenue stream for a UK LTD education consultancy is commission from UK degree referrals — when a student you advise enrols in a UK university, the university may pay you a commission.

**How commission works:**

| Model | How it works |
|-------|-------------|
| **Per-student commission** | University pays a commission per recruited student who enrols — typically after enrolment, sometimes after a certain period. Amount varies — hundreds to thousands of GBP per student. |
| **Commission by course level** | Some universities pay different commission rates for different course levels — undergraduate, Master's, PhD, foundation, etc. |
| **Commission by country/region** | Some universities have different commission structures for different territories or agents. |
| **Retainer + commission** | Some universities pay a retainer (annual fee) to agents in addition to or instead of per-student commission. |
| **Scholarship commission** | Some universities offer commission on scholarship-supported students — or reduced commission if the student has a scholarship. |

**Factors affecting commission:**

- **University** — each university sets its own commission rates and policies.
- **Course** — some courses may have higher or lower commission (e.g., high-demand courses, postgraduate courses, MBA).
- **Agent agreement** — commission is set in the agent agreement — negotiate with universities.
- **Student enrolment** — commission usually paid after enrolment, sometimes after a period (e.g., 3-6 months into the course).
- **Ethics** — reputable universities and agents follow ethical recruitment standards — commission should not compromise student interests. Students should get good advice, not be pushed into unsuitable courses for commission.

**Commission revenue model for a consultancy:**

- **Volume-based** — more students recruited = more commission. Build a student pipeline.
- **High-value courses** — postgraduate courses, MBAs, high-demand courses may have higher commission or higher tuition (and some commission models are percentage-based).
- **Multiple university partnerships** — partner with many universities — more options for students, more commission opportunities.
- **Retention and referrals** — students who are happy refer other students — word of mouth, repeat business.

**For non-resident education consultants:**

Your UK LTD receives commission from UK universities in GBP — into your UK LTD bank account. This is clean, professional, scalable income. As you build university partnerships and a student pipeline, commission revenue grows.

## Step 6: Building a Student Pipeline — Sourcing and Counselling Students

The core of a study visa consultancy business is students — building a pipeline of students seeking UK study, advising them, and supporting them through the application and visa process.

**Building a student pipeline:**

| Approach | How it works |

| **School and college partnerships** | Partner with schools, colleges, universities in your home country — they refer students seeking UK study. Guidance counsellors, teachers, administrators. |

**Student counselling — key elements:**

## Step 7: UKVI Compliance and Ethical Recruitment — Doing It Right

Study visa consultancies must operate ethically and in compliance with UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration) regulations and ethical recruitment standards.

**UKVI compliance:**

| Area | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Accurate visa advice** | Provide accurate, up-to-date information on UK study visas. Do not guarantee visas. Do not advise students to provide false information. |
| **Approved sponsors** | Only advise students to apply to approved UK sponsors (universities and colleges with Student Route sponsor licence). Check sponsor status. |
| **CAS accuracy** | Ensure CAS information is correct — students should not apply with incorrect CAS information. |
| **Financial requirement accuracy** | Advise students accurately on financial requirements — how much, what funds are acceptable, 28-day rule. |
| **English language accuracy** | Advise accurately on English language requirements, accepted tests, scores, exemptions. |
| **Documentation** | Advise students on required documents, authenticity, translations, notarisation where needed. |
| **Visa application integrity** | Ensure students apply honestly, with accurate information and genuine documents. No fraud, no deception. |
| **Regulatory awareness** | Stay informed of UKVI rules, policy changes, guidance.Compliance with immigration laws. |

**Ethical recruitment standards:**

| Standard | Relevance |
|----------|-----------|
| **British Council Agent Code of Conduct** | Code of conduct for education agents recruiting students to the UK — ethical standards, student-centred approach, transparency, compliance. |
| **UCAS Agent Code of Practice** | Code of practice for agents involved in UCAS applications — ethical standards, student interests, transparency. |
| **Agent accreditation** | Some organisations accredit education agents — e.g., ICEF, some university agent accreditation programmes. Accreditation shows commitment to standards. |
| **Student-centred approach** | Advice should be in the student's best interest — suitable courses, suitable universities, honest advice, no pushing unsuitable options for commission. |
| **Transparency** | Be clear with students about your role, your relationships with universities, any commission, fees you charge. |
| **Confidentiality** | Respect student information — personal data, application information, visa information. GDPR compliance for student data. |

**OISC and immigration advice:**

- OISC (Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner) regulates immigration advice in the UK. If you are providing immigration advice (including visa advice) in the UK, you may need to be regulated by OISC or be exempt (e.g., qualified lawyers).
- If you are based outside the UK and advising students on UK visas, you need to understand the rules — providing immigration advice regulated by UK law may have implications.
- Be clear about what you are providing — general information and guidance vs formal immigration advice. If in doubt, refer students to OISC-registered advisers or qualified immigration lawyers for formal advice.
