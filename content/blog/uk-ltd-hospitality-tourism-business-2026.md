# UK LTD for Hospitality & Tourism Business

If you want to start or run a hospitality and tourism business — hotel, B&B, restaurant, cafe, bar, pub, tour operator, travel agency, destination management, event tourism, MICE, online travel, holiday lets, or attractions — a UK LTD gives you a professional company structure with limited liability, VAT handling, multi-currency banking, licensing capability, and credibility. Works whether you are based in the UK or operating from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, UAE, Turkey, China, and beyond.

---

## Why a UK LTD for Hospitality & Tourism

Hospitality and tourism businesses deal with premises, customers, food and beverage, alcohol, staff, property, bookings, international visitors, and multi-currency transactions. A UK LTD provides:

| Without LTD | With UK LTD |
|-------------|-------------|
| Personal liability for premises risks, customer injury, food poisoning, alcohol incidents | UK LTD = limited liability |
| Sole trader — less professional credibility with customers, suppliers, landlords, banks | UK LTD = registered company — professional credibility |
| Hard to open business bank accounts for hospitality/tourism income | UK LTD = recognised entity for business banking |
| Multi-currency transactions (international tourists, online bookings, suppliers) | UK LTD multi-currency account (Wise/Revolut) |
| VAT complexity on room rates (reduced rate), food & beverage (standard rate), alcohol (standard rate), catering | UK LTD VAT registration — proper hospitality VAT framework |
| Alcohol licensing (premises licence + personal licence for bar, pub, restaurant with alcohol) | UK LTD = recognised entity for premises licence; personal licence holder needed |
| Food hygiene registration and food safety compliance (if serving food) | UK LTD = recognised food business entity |
| Property — hotel buildings, restaurant premises, B&B properties, hospitality real estate | UK LTD = recognised entity for commercial leases, property purchase, business rates |
| Staff — reception, housekeeping, waiting staff, kitchen, bar, tour guides, drivers | UK LTD = employer entity — contracts, wages, payroll, employers' liability insurance (legal requirement) |
| Scaling — more rooms, outlets, tour products, agencies, online channels | UK LTD = scalable base |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs**, a UK LTD is the bridge to run a hospitality and tourism business in the UK with a registered UK company.

## Step 2: Hospitality Property — Premises, Planning, Business Rates

Hospitality businesses often involve property — hotel buildings, restaurant premises, B&B properties, pub premises. Property considerations are significant.

| Consideration | Notes |
|---------------|-------|
| **Own vs lease** | Own the property (purchase) or lease (commercial lease) — different capital, risk, flexibility |
| **Type of premises** | Hotel building, restaurant unit, B&B property (often residential/commercial mix), pub premises, cafe unit, tour office, etc. |
| **Planning permission** | Check planning for the business use — change of use may be needed (residential to commercial for B&B, office to restaurant), some uses restricted. Check with local authority planning. |
| **Use class** | Hotels (use class C1 or sui generis), B&Bs (sui generis or C1 depending), restaurants (sui generis), pubs (sui generis) — check appropriate use class |
| **Business rates** | Non-domestic rates (business rates) apply to commercial hospitality premises (hotels, restaurants, pubs). B&Bs may be rateable — small B&Bs may be exempt if mainly residential. Check rateable value, reliefs (small business rates relief) |
| **Licensing** | Alcohol licence (premises licence) for premises selling alcohol — required for bar, pub, restaurant serving alcohol. Late-night refreshment licence (hot food/drink late at night, 11pm-5am, some areas). Short-term lets regulation in some areas |
| **Building/fire safety** | Premises must meet building regulations, fire safety, accessibility, licensing conditions, health and safety |
| **Tourism accommodation regulations** | Some tourist accommodations may need registration/notification with local authority, safety requirements (fire safety) |
| **Short-term lets regulations** | In some areas, short-term lets (Airbnb-style) may be subject to regulation, planning restrictions, licensing — check local authority rules |

**Premises checklist:**

- Planning — check planning permission and use class for business type
- Lease/purchase — terms, deposit, rent/rates, length, break clauses
- Licensing — alcohol licence (if applicable), late-night refreshment licence, any other licences
- Building/fire safety — meet regulations, fire risk assessment
- Health and safety — premises risk assessment
- Accessibility — Equality Act considerations
- Insurance — buildings, contents, public liability, business interruption, employers' liability (if employing)
- Business rates — rateable value, reliefs, payment

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

If you are based abroad and running a UK hospitality business, you may need UK premises (hotel, restaurant, B&B, pub) or operate online (travel agency, tour booking) or from home (tour office). If leasing/purchasing premises, your UK LTD is the tenant/buyer. Consider whether physical premises are needed or start online and expand later.

## Step 3: VAT on Hospitality — Room Rates, Food & Beverage, Alcohol

VAT on hospitality is complex — different supplies have different rates.

| Supply | VAT treatment | Notes |
|--------|---------------|-------|
| **Hotel/accommodation room rates** | Generally reduced rate (5%) | Residential accommodation (hotel, B&B, guest house, holiday let) — room rates generally reduced-rated (5%), subject to conditions. Long stays (28+ days) may be exempt |
| **Hotel services (other than room)** | Standard rate (20%) | Room service, hotel restaurant, hotel bar — standard-rated |
| **B&B breakfast** | Standard rate (20%) | Food and beverage — standard-rated. B&B = accommodation (reduced) + breakfast (standard) — mixed supplies |
| **Restaurant food and beverage** | Standard rate (20%) | Food and non-alcoholic drinks in restaurants — standard-rated |
| **Catering** | Standard rate (20%) | Catering services — standard-rated |
| **Alcohol** | Standard rate (20%) | Alcohol in bar/pub/restaurant — standard-rated |
| **Hot takeaway food** | Zero-rated (0%) in some cases | Certain hot takeaway food may be zero-rated (specific conditions) — but most restaurant food is standard-rated |
| **Cold takeaway food** | Zero-rated (0%) in some cases | Certain cold takeaway food may be zero-rated (specific conditions) |
| **Certain food items** | Zero-rated or standard-rated depending on food | Basic food items (bread, milk, vegetables, fruit, meat, etc.) generally zero-rated — but prepared food, restaurant meals, hot food are standard-rated. Specific rules — complex |
| **Entertainment** | Some standard-rated, some exempt | Admission to entertainment — varies |
| **Travel/tourism services** | Standard-rated or outside scope depending on service | Travel agency, tour operation — place of supply rules (B2B vs B2C), may be outside UK VAT or standard-rated. Complex for travel |

**Key VAT points:**

- **Reduced rate accommodation** — hotel, B&B, guest house, holiday let room rates generally reduced-rated (5%)
- **Standard rate food and beverage** — most food and beverage in hospitality is standard-rated (20%)
- **Mixed supplies** — B&B = accommodation (reduced) + breakfast (standard)
- **Alcohol** — standard-rated (20%)

**VAT registration:**

- **Mandatory** — over £90,000 rolling 12-month turnover
- **Voluntary** — below threshold, can register voluntarily — reclaim VAT on business costs (property costs, kitchen equipment, FF&E for hotel, food/beverage stock, alcohol stock, marketing, professional fees)

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD, if VAT-registered, charges VAT on room rates (reduced rate), food and beverage (standard rate), alcohol (standard rate), catering correctly. For online bookings globally, multi-currency banking (Wise/Revolut) handles international payments.

## Step 4: Alcohol Licensing — Premises Licence and Personal Licence

If the hospitality business serves alcohol — bar, pub, restaurant with alcohol, B&B with bar, hotel bar — alcohol licensing is required.

| Licence | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Premises licence** | Licence for the premises authorising sale of alcohol, late-night refreshment, regulated entertainment (live music, performance) — granted by local authority (council). Required for any premises selling alcohol |
| **Personal licence** | Personal licence held by an individual authorising them to authorise sale of alcohol on licensed premises — granted by local authority. At least one personal licence holder needed. Applicant must be fit and proper person |

**Key points:**

- **Premises licence** — applied for by the company (UK LTD can apply, with a designated premises supervisor who holds a personal licence). Sets out licensable activities, opening hours, conditions
- **Personal licence** — held by an individual — designated premises supervisor (DPS) for alcohol sales should hold a personal licence
- **Licensing objectives** — prevention of crime and disorder, public safety, prevention of public nuisance, protection of children from harm
- **Licensing conditions** — premises licence may have conditions — opening hours, security, noise, etc.
- **Licensing authority** — local council handles applications and hearings

**For the UK LTD:**

- UK LTD can apply for premises licence (as the company)
- An individual (director, manager, or other) with a personal licence should be the DPS for alcohol sales
- If UK LTD owns the pub/restaurant/bar, the company applies for premises licence; a personal licence holder is the DPS

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

If you are a non-resident entrepreneur running a UK bar, pub, restaurant with alcohol, the UK LTD applies for the premises licence. You (or a trusted individual in the UK) need a personal licence as DPS. If you are abroad, you may need a UK-based individual to hold the personal licence and act as DPS. This is important — alcohol licensing requires a personal licence holder in the UK.

## Step 5: Food Hygiene and Food Business Registration

If the hospitality business serves food — restaurant, cafe, B&B with breakfast, pub with food, hotel restaurant, catering — food hygiene and food business registration are required.

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Food business registration** | Register food business with local authority — required before starting to trade (at least 28 days before). Free to register. Covers premises where food is handled, prepared, served |
| **Food hygiene** | Comply with food hygiene regulations — hygiene of food preparation, storage, handling — keep food safe, prevent contamination. Follow good hygiene practices |
| **Food Safety Act 1990 / Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013** | Legal framework for food safety — compliance, due diligence |
| **Food Standards Agency (FSA)** | UK food safety regulator — guidance, standards |
| **Food hygiene rating scheme (FHRS)** | Local authorities inspect food businesses and give a rating (0-5) — displayed (England, Wales, Northern Ireland; Scotland has its own scheme). Higher rating = better. Important for customer confidence |
| **Food safety management** | Have a food safety management system — HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) principles — record keeping, procedures |
| **Allergen information** | Provide allergen information — 14 major allergens (celery, cereals with gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphur dioxide/sulphites). Menu labelling, information to customers |
| **Staff training** | Food hygiene training for food handling staff — level 2 food hygiene certificate (or equivalent) commonly required/recommended |
| **Premises hygiene** | Clean premises, adequate facilities (sinks, washing, storage, refrigeration), pest control |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD, as a food business (restaurant, cafe, B&B with breakfast, pub with food, hotel restaurant, catering), must register with local authority, comply with food hygiene regulations, have food safety management system, provide allergen information, undergo hygiene inspections (food hygiene rating). Staff handling food should have food hygiene training. Essential for food-serving hospitality businesses.

## Step 7: Staff and Employment in Hospitality & Tourism

Hospitality and tourism businesses are people-intensive — employing reception staff, housekeeping, waiting staff, kitchen staff, bar staff, tour guides, drivers, etc.

| Consideration | Notes |
|---------------|-------|
| **Employment contracts** | Written statements of employment particulars (required by law), employment contracts, terms and conditions |
| **Wages** | Minimum wage (National Living Wage / National Minimum Wage) — must pay at least minimum wage. Record hours, pay properly |
| **Payroll** | Payroll — pay wages, deduct tax and National Insurance (PAYE), report to HMRC, provide payslips. Use payroll service or software |
| **Employers' liability insurance** | LEGAL REQUIREMENT if you employ anyone — cover for employee injury/illness at work. Must have at least £5 million cover |
| **Working time** | Working time regulations — rest breaks, maximum hours, etc. |
| **Pensions** | Auto-enrolment — if eligible employees, must enrol in workplace pension (subject to criteria) |
| **Right to work** | Check employees' right to work in the UK (for UK employees; for non-UK staff, visa/work permit requirements) |
| **Hospitality-specific staff** | Reception, housekeeping, waiting staff, kitchen/chefs, bar staff, tour guides, drivers, activity leaders, event staff — appropriate roles, training, uniforms |
| **Tour guides** | If tour operation — tour guides may need specific knowledge, skills, possibly licensing (certain tours/guides in some areas), first aid |
| **Customer-facing staff** | Hospitality is customer-facing — staff training in customer service, hospitality, professionalism, product knowledge (for tours) |
| **Employment law** | Compliance with employment law — contracts, wages, working time, holidays, discrimination, health and safety, etc. |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD, if employing staff, must comply with UK employment law — contracts, minimum wage, payroll (PAYE), employers' liability insurance (legal requirement), working time, pensions (auto-enrolment where applicable), right to work checks. Hospitality/tourism staff may be UK-based or (if staff from abroad) need appropriate visa/work permission. Getting employment right is essential — legal compliance, staff satisfaction, customer experience.

## Step 8: Tourism Industry Bodies, Regulations and Considerations

Hospitality and tourism businesses should be aware of industry bodies, regulations, and considerations.

| Area | Details |
|------|---------|
| **ATOL (Air Travel Organisers' Licensing)** | If you are a travel agent or tour operator selling flight-inclusive travel packages (holidays including flights), you may need ATOL protection — ATOL bonding/licensing — to protect customers if the business fails. ATOL is required by law for certain travel sellers. Check if your travel business requires ATOL |
| **ABTA** | Association of British Travel Agents — travel industry association — membership, guidance, code of conduct, financial protection (if applicable), support |
| **Tourism boards / local tourism** | Local tourism organisations, destination marketing organisations, tourism boards — partnership, marketing, local tourism promotion |
| **Accommodation registration/notification** | Some tourist accommodations may need to register or notify the local authority (e.g., tourist accommodation registration in some areas). Check local authority requirements |
| **Short-term lets regulation** | In some areas, short-term lets (Airbnb, etc.) may be subject to regulation — planning restrictions, licensing, registration — check local rules |
| **Travel regulations** | Travel and tourism regulations — consumer protection for travel packages (Package Travel Regulations), ATOL, financial protection, cancellation rights. Travel sellers need to comply with package travel regulations if selling package holidays |
| **Consumer protection** | Consumer Rights Act, Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 (if selling travel packages), unfair terms, pricing transparency |
| **Data protection (GDPR)** | Customer data — bookings, guest details, travel information — GDPR compliance, privacy policy, data security |
| **Equality Act** | Accessibility and non-discrimination — consider accessibility for customers with disabilities, non-discrimination in service provision |
| **Insurance** | Public liability insurance (essential for hospitality — customers on premises, tours), travel insurance (if providing travel), property insurance, employers' liability (if employing), business interruption, etc. |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD should comply with relevant hospitality/tourism regulations — ATOL if selling flight-inclusive packages, food hygiene, alcohol licensing, planning, consumer protection (including Package Travel Regulations if selling travel packages), GDPR, insurance. Industry bodies like ABTA can provide guidance and support. This protects your business, customers, and ensures compliance.
