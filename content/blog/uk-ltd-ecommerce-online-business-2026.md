## Step 4: VAT on E-Commerce — Understanding Your VAT Obligations

VAT is a key consideration for e-commerce and online businesses in the UK. The VAT treatment depends on what you sell (goods or digital services), who you sell to (UK or overseas customers), and how you sell (own store, marketplaces, etc.).

**VAT basics for online businesses:**

| Scenario | VAT treatment |
|----------|----------------|
| **Selling physical goods in the UK (to UK customers)** | Standard UK VAT (20%) applies — charge VAT on sales, include in VAT return, pay to HMRC. If VAT-registered. |
| **Selling physical goods to EU customers (post-Brexit)** | Generally, you do not charge UK VAT on exports to EU (exports are zero-rated, subject to evidence). EU customer may pay import VAT/duty when goods arrive in EU — depends on value, Incoterm, and how goods are shipped. VAT-registered EU customers may reclaim. |
| **Selling digital products/services to UK customers** | UK VAT (20%) applies on digital services sold to UK customers — charge VAT, report in VAT return. |
| **Selling digital products/services to EU consumers (B2C)** | VAT rules for digital services to EU consumers — you may need to charge VAT in the customer's EU country (using EU VAT OSS / one-stop shop, or UK's equivalent for UK businesses selling to EU). Rules changed post-Brexit. |
| **Selling digital products/services to EU businesses (B2B)** | Generally, reverse charge — EU business accounts for VAT in their country. You issue invoice without UK VAT, customer's VAT number on invoice. |
| **Selling to customers outside UK and EU** | Generally, no UK VAT on sales to non-UK/non-EU customers (exports of services/goods). |
| **Dropshipping — importing goods into UK for sale** | If goods are imported into UK (from overseas supplier to UK customer or to UK warehouse), import VAT applies on import — VAT-registered business can usually reclaim import VAT through VAT return. |
| **Dropshipping — direct from overseas supplier to overseas customer** | If goods go direct from overseas supplier to customer (e.g., China supplier to US customer), with UK LTD as intermediary, the UK LTD may not physically import goods — depends on arrangement. Import VAT may or may not apply. |

**VAT registration for e-commerce businesses:**

- **VAT registration threshold** — currently £90,000 turnover (rolling 12 months) — businesses over this must register for VAT.
- **Voluntary registration** — businesses under the threshold can register voluntarily — useful for reclaiming VAT on costs (e.g., import VAT on dropshipping goods, VAT on platform fees, VAT on business expenses), and for credibility.
- **VAT on digital services** — even if under threshold, selling digital services to EU consumers may require VAT registration for EU VAT purposes. UK businesses selling digital services to EU consumers may need to register for EU VAT OSS.

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

If you are an online business owner operating from outside UK, a UK LTD allows you to:
- Sell online to UK customers — charge UK VAT if VAT-registered.
- Sell digital products/services globally — handle VAT on digital services correctly.
- Run a dropshipping business — handle import VAT, customs for imported goods.
- Use multi-currency banking — receive and manage payments in multiple currencies.

A UK LTD gives the structure to operate an online business professionally, with proper VAT handling.

## Step 5: E-Commerce Legal Requirements — Staying Compliant

Online businesses must comply with legal requirements — consumer rights, distance selling, data protection, pricing, returns, product safety, etc.

**Key legal areas for e-commerce:**

| Area | What it involves |
|------|------------------|
| **Consumer Rights Act 2015** | Rights of consumers buying goods, digital content, and services online — goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described; digital content must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described; services must be performed with reasonable care and skill. |
| **Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013** (Distance Selling Regulations) | Requirements for selling to consumers online — information requirements (identity, contact, description of goods/services, price, payment, delivery, cancellation rights, etc.), 14-day cancellation/right of withdrawal for most goods and services bought online (with exceptions), refund requirements. |
| **GDPR (UK)** | Data protection — handling personal data of customers, website visitors, etc. — lawful basis, transparency, security, data subject rights, etc. Privacy policy, cookies (Cookie Policy + cookie consent), data processing. |
| **Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR)** | Marketing by email, SMS, cookies — consent requirements for marketing, cookies. |
| **Pricing regulations** | Clear pricing — inclusive of VAT (or stating +VAT), total price, no misleading pricing. |
| **Returns and refunds** | Policy on returns and refunds — complying with Consumer Contracts Regulations (14-day cancellation), Consumer Rights Act (goods not as described, etc.), and any voluntary returns policy. |
| **Product safety and regulations** | Products sold must meet applicable safety and regulatory standards — e.g., UKCA/CE marking (where applicable), product regulations, restricted/dangerous goods, etc. |
| **IP and content** | Ensure you have rights to use products, images, descriptions, brand names — avoid IP infringement (trademarks, copyright, etc.). |
| **Terms and conditions / privacy policy** | Clear website terms — terms of sale, privacy policy, cookie policy, etc. |
| **Accessible websites** | Considerations for website accessibility — though not always legally required, good practice and may be required for public sector. |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD must comply with UK consumer law, data protection, and other regulations when selling online to UK customers. Get proper terms and conditions, privacy policy, and comply with consumer rights, distance selling, and returns requirements. If selling globally, consider regulations in other countries too.

## Step 6: Currency Management for Online Sales — Receiving in Multiple Currencies

Online businesses selling globally receive payments in multiple currencies. Managing these efficiently matters — FX costs can eat into margins.

**Currency management for e-commerce:**

| Strategy | How it helps |
|----------|------------|
| **Multi-currency business account (Wise Business / Revolut Business)** | Receive payments in local currencies (GBP, USD, EUR, AED, etc.) — hold balances in each currency, convert only when needed, at lower FX rates than traditional banks. |
| **Local currency receiving details** | Wise Business provides local bank details in multiple currencies — customers can pay via local bank transfer in their currency, reducing costs. |
| **Charge in customer's currency** | Where possible, charge customers in their currency — improves customer experience, may increase conversion. But consider FX risk. |
| **Convert at favourable times** | Monitor currency rates — convert when rates are favourable, if you have flexibility. |
| **natURAL hedging** | Match currencies — if you earn in USD, try to pay suppliers in USD where possible. |
| **Platform currency settings** | Set your store's currency(s) appropriately — Shopify, WooCommerce allow multiple currencies (via apps or built-in). |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD is the entity receiving online sales revenue — Wise Business or Revolut Business lets you receive in multiple currencies, hold balances, convert efficiently, and pay expenses/suppliers in appropriate currencies. This is essential for a global online business.
