## Step-by-Step: Registering a UK LTD from France

### Step 1: Choose Your Company Name

Check name availability on the [Companies House register](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/). Your name must end in "Limited" or "Ltd" and cannot be identical to an existing registered name.

Many French founders choose English names for international appeal, or bilingual names that work in both French and English markets.

### Step 2: Prepare Your ACSP Identity Verification

Under the **ECCTA 2026 rules**, every director and person with significant control (PSC) must undergo identity verification through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP).

French applicants need:
- **Valid French passport** (preferred) or Carte d'identité
- **Justificatif de domicile** (utility bill, tax notice, or bank statement — dated within 3 months)
- **Biometric verification** — done remotely via webcam or smartphone

The verification process takes **2-5 minutes** and is fully digital.

### Step 3: Decide on Directors and Shareholders

A UK LTD requires:
- **At least one director** (can be the same person as the shareholder)
- **At least one shareholder** (can be the same person as the director)
- A **Person with Significant Control (PSC)** — usually the same person

French founders can be both director and sole shareholder. There is **no requirement for a UK-resident director**.

### Step 4: Choose Your Registered Office Address

Options for French founders:
- **London virtual office** — prestigious WC1, W1, or EC1 address from £99/year
- **Director's service address** — keeps your French home address off the public record
- **Physical UK office** — if you have one (rare for remote founders)

> **Important:** You cannot use a PO Box as your registered office.

### Step 5: File Your Incorporation

Your ACSP provider (that's us) files with Companies House online. Timeline:

- **Same day** for straightforward applications
- **Up to 24 hours** on busy days
- **Typically 3-6 hours**

Documents received:
- **Certificate of Incorporation** (PDF)
- **Company number**
- **Memorandum and Articles of Association**
- **Share certificates** (digital)

### Step 6: Register for Corporation Tax

Within 3 months of starting business activity, register for Corporation Tax with HMRC. Your LTD receives a **Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)** by post within 14 days.

French founders should also consider:
- **VAT registration** if taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 (2026 threshold)
- **PAYE registration** if hiring UK employees
- **Dividend strategy** — post-tax profit distributions

## Tax Considerations for French Founders

### UK Corporation Tax vs French Impôt sur les Sociétés

A UK LTD pays Corporation Tax on profits (19-25%). A French SARL or SAS pays Impôt sur les Sociétés (IRES) at 25% plus potentially social charges on distributed profits. For many French founders with international revenue, the UK LTD structure can deliver a lower effective tax rate, especially when combined with the France-UK Double Taxation Agreement.

### France-UK Double Taxation Agreement

The [France-UK Double Taxation Agreement](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/portail/particulier/je-suis-non-résident/impôts-en-cas-de-domiciliation-en-france/conventions-avoid-double-imposition) prevents double taxation. Key points:

- **Dividends** paid from a UK LTD to a French resident are typically subject to a **withholding tax** (5-15% depending on the structure)
- **Business profits** are generally taxable only in the country where the company is managed and controlled
- **French founders** should consult a cross-border tax professional (expert-comptable)

### "Siège social" and Place of Management Risk

French tax law may treat a UK LTD as a French tax resident if its "siège social" (registered office/head office) is effectively in France or its "place of effective management" is in France. If all your directors make decisions from Paris, Lyon, or Marseille, the French tax authorities could argue the company is French for tax purposes.

**Mitigation:**
- Hold board meetings outside France when possible
- Document decision-making processes clearly
- Consider having at least one director based outside France
- Seek professional French tax advice (expert-comptable)
