# UK LTD for Concert Promoters and Event Organizers: The 2026 Complete Legal Setup Guide

If you are a DJ, promoter, or event organizer — whether you are based in London or operating from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, UAE, Nigeria, or anywhere else in the world — a UK LTD is the single legal entity that lets you book UK venues, sign talent contracts, receive ticket payments, get event permits, and insure your events under one business structure.

This guide covers exactly how that works, step by step, from formation through your first event.

---

## Why a UK LTD Matters for Concert Promoters

Running events without a registered business is possible informally, but it limits everything:

| What you cannot do without a UK LTD | What a UK LTD lets you do |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Sign venue contracts professionally | A UK LTD is the contracting entity venues trust |
| Receive large ticket payments | A UK LTD bank account (Wise/Revolut) receives GBP from box office, sponsors, and ticket platforms |
| Contract UK-based DJs, MCs, bands | A UK LTD employs or contracts UK talent legally |
| Apply for council event permits | Many UK councils require a registered business to issue event licenses |
| Get event liability insurance | UK LTD = insurable entity for public liability, cancellation, equipment |
| Sponsor artists or run a record label | UK LTD = legal entity for sponsorship deals, advances, royalty collection |
| Get paid from international promoters | A UK LTD receiving GBP, USD, EUR from overseas co-promoters |

For non-resident promoters, a UK LTD is even more critical — it is the bridge that lets you operate in the UK music scene from abroad.

## Step 1: Form Your UK LTD

Standard UK LTD formation. For concert promoters, the SIC code that matters most is:

| SIC Code | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| 90.01 | Performers, sportspersons and related artists |
| 90.02 | Support activities for performing arts |
| 90.03 | Artistic creation |
| 90.04 | Operation of arts facilities and auxiliary products. |

**Most promoters use 90.02** — "Support activities for performing arts" — as their primary code. It covers event production, talent management, and performance support.

## Step 3: Signing Venue Contracts — What Promoters Need to Know

UK venues — from warehouses and clubs to theatres and outdoor sites — work with registered businesses. Here is what a venue contract typically requires:

| Contract element | What it means |
|------------------|---------------|
| **Promoter entity** | Your UK LTD name, company number, registered address |
| **Venue hire fee** | The venue's charge — flat fee or percentage of ticket revenue |
| **Date and time** | Loading, soundcheck, doors, event, clearance times |
| **Capacity and ticketing** | Maximum capacity, ticket price, ticketing platform |
| **Technical requirements** | Sound, lights, power, stage, rigging, visas (for international acts) |
| **Licences** | Premises licence held by venue, any temporary event notice (TEN) needed |
| **Insurance** | Public liability insurance certificate from promoter |
| **Security** | Whether venue provides security or promoter must arrange |
| **Bar and ticket split** | How ticket revenue and bar sales are shared |
| **Cancellation clause** | What happens if event is cancelled, who bears the cost |
| **Force majeure** | Acts of God, extreme weather, regulatory changes |

**Non-resident promoters:** Your UK LTD is the contracting entity. The venue contract is between the venue and your UK LTD, not you personally. This gives you limited liability — if something goes wrong with the event, your personal assets are protected.

## Step 4: Event Permits and Licences

Depending on the type and scale of your event, you may need:
**Licensing:**

| Licence | When it applies | Who issues it |
|---------|----------------|---------------|
| **Premises licence** | Venue already licensed for music, alcohol, late opening | Local council (borough) |
| **Temporary Event Notice (TEN)** | One-off event, up to 499 people, limited hours | Local council — give 10 working days notice |
| **Music Licence (PRS/PPL)** | If playing recorded music or live music in public | PRS for Music / PPL |
| **PACE licence** | If hiring security staff | Security Industry Authority (SIA) |
| **Street collection licence** | If collecting donations outside venue | Local council |
| **Street trading licence** | If selling merchandise outside venue | Local council |

**Promoter responsibilities:**

- Confirm the venue has the right premises licence for your event type
- If the venue does not have a licence covering your event, you may need a TEN
- For outdoor events, additional planning permission from the council may be required
- Notify the local council of your event — many councils have event notification forms
- Check noise conditions on the venue's licence — some venues have noise limits or curfews

**Ten key facts for non-resident promoters:**

1. A TEN is cheap (£25-£100) and covers events up to 499 people
2. You can give a TEN as the promoter or the venue can give it
3. The TEN covers alcohol, regulated entertainment, and late-night refreshment
4. For events over 500 people, a full premises licence application is needed
5. Outdoor festivals need more complex licensing — start early
6. PRS/PPL licences are often handled by the venue, but confirm
7. Security staff must hold SIA licences — your UK LTD can hire an SIA-licensed security company
8. If you hire security directly, your UK LTD may need its own SIA licence
9. Notify the local council even if the venue has a licence — good practice
10. Venues will ask for your public liability insurance certificate — see Step 5

## Step 5: Event Insurance — What You Must Insure

Event insurance is not optional for professional promoters. Venues will not sign a contract without it.

**Types of event insurance:**

| Insurance type | What it covers | Typical cost |
|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| **Public Liability** | Injury to third parties, damage to property caused by event | £150-£500 per event |
| **Cancellation** | Lost revenue if event is cancelled (weather, artist illness, venue issues) | Percentage of event value |
| **Equipment** | Theft, damage to sound, lights, stage equipment | Premiums based on equipment value |
| **Employer's Liability** | Required if you employ staff (mandatory £5m cover) | ~£50-£150/year |
| **Personal Accident** | Injury to performers, crew, staff | Premiums based on headcount |
| **Ticket liability** | Refund costs if event is cancelled after tickets sold | Based on ticket revenue |
| **Cyber** | Ticket platform hacks, data breaches | Growing importance |

**How to get event insurance as a UK LTD:**

1. Your UK LTD is the policyholder
2. Provide your company number, event details, capacity, venue, activities
3. Get a certificate before signing the venue contract (venues ask for this)
4. Keep certificates for each event — venues and councils may ask to see them

**Recommended approach:** Use a specialist event insurance broker rather than a general insurer. They understand concert, festival, and gig insurance. Some UK insurers specialise in the music events sector.

## Step 6: Talent Contracts — Booking DJs, Bands, MCs, and Acts

Your UK LTD contracts talent. The contract is between your UK LTD (promoter) and the artist/agency.

**What a talent contract covers:**

| Contract element | What it means |
|------------------|---------------|
| **Act name and details** | Artist, agency, contact, technical rider |
| **Performance date and time** | Load-in, soundcheck, set time, load-out |
| **Fee** | Fee in GBP (or other currency) — fixed fee or percentage of ticket |
| **Payment terms** | Deposit on signing, balance on performance (or within X days) |
| **Travel and accommodation** | Who pays for travel, hotel, per diem — specify clearly |
| **Technical requirements** | Sound system, lights, instruments, monitoring, stage plot |
| ** Hospitality** | Dressing room, rider items (food, drinks, merchandise) |
| **Merchandise** | Who sells merch, split of revenue, space at venue |
| **Recording rights** | Can you record the set? For what purpose? |
| **Cancellation** | What fee is payable if artist cancels, what notice is required |
| **Force majeure** | Illness, travel disruption, extreme weather |
| **Insurance** | Artist may require you to hold certain insurance |
| **Governing law** | English law, jurisdiction England & Wales |

**Non-resident promoters booking UK talent:**

Your UK LTD contracts the UK DJ or band. The contract is in English law. Payment is from your UK LTD bank account to the artist's UK bank account (or international account). This is clean, professional, and protects both sides.

**Tips for working with UK talent:**

- Use a clear technical rider — specify what you provide vs what the artist provides
- Confirm visa requirements for international acts — UK visa for non-UK/EEA artists performing in UK
- Pay on time — UK artists and agencies expect payment within agreed terms (usually 7-30 days)
- Keep contracts in writing — email confirmations are not enough for professional bookings
- For recurring talent (resident DJs at a venue), use a simple recurring contract

## Step 7: Ticket Sales and Revenue

How ticket money flows for a UK LTD promoter:

```
Ticket buyers → Ticket platform (e.g., Eventbrite, Dice, Skiddle, LPM)
    → Ticket platform pays your UK LTD bank account
    → Your UK LTD pays: venue hire, talent fees, security, insurance, marketing
    → Profit = ticket revenue minus event costs
```

**Ticket platforms operating in the UK:**

| Platform | Best for | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| **Eventbrite** | General events, all sizes | Most widely recognized, good for international audiences |
| **Dice** | Club nights, DJ events, music gigs | Popular with younger audiences, digital tickets |
| **Skiddle** | UK music events, festivals, gigs | Strong UK music audience, good for regional events |
| **LPM (Live Play Music)** | Club nights, DJ events, electronic music | Popular with electronic/club scene |
| **Dice / Skiddle / LPM** | All have affiliate/referral potential | |
| **Your own ticket site** | Larger promoters, established brands | Requires your own platform + payments |

**Receipt and payment:**

- Ticket platforms pay into your UK LTD bank account (usually 24-48 hours after event, or per contract)
- Keep records of ticket revenue per event — needed for VAT if you exceed threshold, and for bookkeeping
- If you sell over the VAT threshold (£90,000 per year from 2026), you must register for VAT

**Sponsorship revenue:**

- Sponsors pay your UK LTD directly (bank transfer, cheque)
- Sponsorship agreements are contracts between sponsor and your UK LTD
- Sponsorship income is taxable — report in your UK LTD accounts
- Keep sponsorship contracts on file

## Step 10: Building Your Promotion Brand — From First Event to Recurring Business

A UK LTD gives you a brand. That brand can grow from a single event to a recurring promotion business.

**Growth path for a UK LTD promoter:**

1. **First event** — one gig, one venue, one night. Prove the model.
2. **Recurring nights** — same venue, same night each week/month. Build an audience.
3. **Multiple venues** — expand to different venues in your city.
4. **Multi-city** — expand to other UK cities under your UK LTD brand.
5. **Festival or larger event** — one-off large-scale event, bigger budget, more complexity.
6. **Record label / talent management** — use your UK LTD to sign and develop talent, release music.
7. **International co-promotion** — your UK LTD co-presents events in EU, Asia, Middle East with local promoters.
8. **Event production company** — your UK LTD becomes a full production company, handling multiple events, talent, venues, and clients.

**Each step requires your UK LTD to grow with it:**

- More complex contracts
- More staff (hire employees or contractors)
- More banking (higher volumes, multiple currencies)
- More insurance (larger events need more cover)
- More marketing (bigger audiences, higher ticket sales)
- More travel (more venues, more cities)

The UK LTD is the container that holds it all.
