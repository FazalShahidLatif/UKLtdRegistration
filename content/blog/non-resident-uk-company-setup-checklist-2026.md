# Non-Resident UK Company Setup Checklist for 2026

You can own and run a UK company from another country. You do not usually need to live in the UK to become a director.

You do need a clear plan. The company needs a valid name, a registered office, directors, shareholders, people with significant control, and suitable business activity codes.

Use this checklist before you start an online application.

## 1. Check if a UK company fits your plan

A UK limited company is a separate legal person. It can sign contracts, hold money, and trade with customers.

It does not give you a visa. It does not make you a UK tax resident. It does not guarantee a bank account or payment account.

Check your local tax rules before you form a company. You may have tax duties in the country where you live, the country where you manage the business, or the countries where you sell.

Read the official [Companies House formation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation) before you apply.

## 2. Choose and check the company name

Prepare two or three name choices. A name must not be the same as an existing company. It must not be too similar to another name. Some words need approval.

Use the [free company name checker](/company-name-check) to find possible matches. Also search trademarks before you commit to a brand.

A name check is not the same as trademark protection. A name can pass a Companies House check and still conflict with a trademark.

## 3. Prepare director and shareholder details

A private limited company needs at least one director and one shareholder. One person can hold both roles.

Prepare the full legal name, date of birth, nationality, occupation, home address, and service address for each officer. Prepare the share split for each shareholder.

You must also identify each person with significant control, known as a PSC. A person who owns more than 25% of shares or voting rights will often be a PSC.

Use accurate details. Small errors can cause a filing delay or a later correction.

## 4. Complete identity checks

Companies House identity rules are changing under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act. Your formation route may include an identity check.

Keep a valid passport or accepted identity document ready. Follow the provider's instructions. Use your own details and do not share documents through an untrusted channel.

An ACSP can support identity verification and file the application. You can read about [ACSP identity verification](/blog/acsp-identity-verification-2026) before you begin.

## 5. Arrange a registered office

A UK company must have a registered office address. It is the official address for Companies House and other statutory letters.

The address must be in the correct UK jurisdiction. It must be a suitable physical address. A PO Box alone is not enough.

A director service address is different. It is used for a director's public correspondence. A business address is different again. It is used for day-to-day trading mail.

If you live abroad, an address service can help protect your home privacy. Read the [virtual office and address options](/services/virtual-office) and check what mail handling is included.

## 6. Pick suitable SIC codes

SIC codes describe the company's main business activities. Choose codes that match what you plan to do now.

Do not select a code only because it sounds broad. Banks and payment providers may ask about the activity behind the code. A clear business description makes your application easier to understand.

Keep evidence that supports your activity. This may include a website, supplier details, contracts, invoices, or a simple business plan.

## 7. Plan banking before incorporation

A UK company does not receive a bank account automatically. Each bank or payment provider checks the application on its own terms.

Prepare a short answer for these questions:

- Where do customers live?
- What will the company sell?
- Which currencies will it receive?
- Where will work take place?
- Who controls the company?
- What is the expected monthly turnover?

Keep the answers consistent with your company records and website. A formation agent may offer introductions. No agent can promise approval.

Read our [UK business banking guide for non-residents](/blog/business-banking-uk-ltd-non-residents) before you choose a provider.

## 8. Budget for tax and filing work

Formation is one cost. Running the company is another.

Plan for accounts, a confirmation statement, Corporation Tax, VAT if needed, payroll if you employ people, and address renewals. Some companies need an accountant. Others can manage simple records with suitable software and advice.

Tell HMRC when the company starts trading. Check the current rules at [GOV.UK Corporation Tax](https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax).

VAT registration is a separate process. Read the [UK VAT threshold guide](/blog/vat-registration-threshold-uk-2026) and check the current HMRC position.

## 9. Keep a post-formation calendar

Create reminders for every filing date. Record the accounting reference date and confirmation statement date.

Keep company details current. Update Companies House when a director, PSC, registered office, or other reportable detail changes.

Store these items in a secure folder:

- Certificate of incorporation.
- Memorandum and articles.
- Share certificates.
- Companies House authentication details.
- Bank and payment records.
- Tax registrations and notices.

## 10. Choose your application route

You can apply through Companies House. You can also use a formation agent for guided support.

Direct filing may suit a founder who has an address and knows the process. Agent support may suit an overseas founder who needs address help, identity guidance, or filing review.

Compare the route in our [Companies House versus formation agent guide](/blog/companies-house-vs-formation-agent-uk-2026).

## Final checklist

Before you pay or submit, confirm:

- Your name choices are clear.
- Your director and shareholder details are correct.
- Your PSC information is ready.
- Your identity document is valid.
- Your registered office is suitable.
- Your SIC code matches your activity.
- Your banking plan is realistic.
- Your tax and filing calendar is ready.

When you are ready, [start a UK LTD application](/register-a-limited-company-uk) or [contact our support team](/contact) with your questions.

A UK company can be managed from abroad, but it still needs local compliance discipline. Plan the address, identity check, banking, tax, and filing steps before you submit. That simple preparation can prevent avoidable delays.
