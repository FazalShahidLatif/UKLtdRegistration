# UK LTD for Food, Beverage & Restaurant Business: Start a Restaurant, Cafe, Bar, Pub, Food Truck, Catering Business, Mobile Food Service, Cloud Kitchen, Brewery, Distillery, Coffee Shop, or Food & Beverage Company in 2026

If you are a chef, restaurateur, bar owner, caterer, food entrepreneur, beverage producer, coffee shop owner, food truck operator, or anyone who wants to start or run a food, beverage and restaurant business with a professional UK entity — whether you are based in London or operating from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, UAE, Turkey, China, Vietnam, Brazil, or anywhere in the world — a UK LTD is the legal structure that lets you run a restaurant, cafe, bar, pub, food truck, catering business, mobile food service, cloud kitchen, brewery, distillery, coffee shop, or food and beverage company under one company, with limited liability, professional credibility, proper VAT handling, food hygiene compliance, alcohol licensing capability, and multi-currency banking.

This guide covers what a UK LTD means for food, beverage and restaurant businesses.

## Why a UK LTD for Food, Beverage & Restaurant Business

Food, beverage and restaurant businesses involve food hygiene, food safety, alcohol licensing (if serving alcohol), planning (premises use), business rates, employment (staff — cooks, waiting staff, bar staff, etc.), public liability, product liability, multi-currency transactions (international suppliers, tourists, online food orders globally), and regulatory compliance. A UK LTD gives these businesses:

| What a food/beverage/restaurant business without a UK LTD faces | What a UK LTD gives |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| Personal liability for food poisoning, food safety incidents, customer injury, alcohol incidents, property damage, public liability | UK LTD = limited liability — food business risks sit with the company |
| Operating under personal name or informal structure — less professional credibility with customers, suppliers, landlords, banks, local authorities | UK LTD = registered company — professional credibility with customers, suppliers, landlords, banks, local authorities, partners |
| Difficulty opening business bank accounts for food business income | UK LTD = recognised entity for business banking — food sales, catering income, beverage sales, online food orders, wholesale |
| Multi-currency transactions — international suppliers (imported food/beverage ingredients, equipment), international customers/tourists, online food orders globally | UK LTD multi-currency business account (Wise/Revolut) handles food business income and expenses in multiple currencies |
| VAT complexity on food (zero-rated food, standard-rated restaurant meals, hot food, takeaway, alcohol, catering, etc.) | UK LTD VAT registration gives proper VAT framework — understanding zero-rated food, standard-rated prepared food, alcohol, catering, etc. |
| Alcohol licensing (if serving alcohol — bar, pub, restaurant with alcohol, cafe with alcohol) | UK LTD = recognised entity for alcohol licence application — premises licence, personal licence (if needed) |
| Food hygiene registration and food safety compliance (if handling food) | UK LTD = recognised food business entity — food business registration with local authority, food hygiene compliance, food safety management |
| Premises — restaurant, cafe, bar, pub, food truck pitch, cloud kitchen, brewery, etc. — planning, lease, business rates, licensing | UK LTD = recognised entity for premises lease/purchase, planning, business rates, licensing |
| Staff — chefs, cooks, waiting staff, bar staff, kitchen staff, etc. — employment, wages, payroll, staffing | UK LTD = employer entity — employment contracts, wages (minimum wage), payroll (PAYE), employers' liability insurance (legal requirement) |
| Scaling — adding outlets, menus, services, catering, online, wholesale, franchises | UK LTD = scalable base — add outlets, menus, services, catering, online, wholesale, franchises under one company |
| Difficulty building brand trust and customer confidence | UK LTD brand — professional trading name, trust signals, credibility with customers |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs**, a UK LTD is the bridge that lets you run a food, beverage and restaurant business in the UK with a registered UK company — serving UK customers, international customers, or both — with a professional UK entity.

## Step 2: Food Hygiene and Food Business Registration

If the food business handles food — restaurant, cafe, bar with food, pub with food, food truck, catering, cloud kitchen, food manufacturing, etc. — food hygiene and food business registration are required.

**Food business requirements:**

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Food business registration** | Register the food business with the local authority (council) — required before starting to trade (at least 28 days before). Free to register. Covers the premises where food is handled, prepared, served, manufactured, etc. If multiple premises, each may need registration. |
| **Food hygiene** | Comply with food hygiene regulations — hygiene of food preparation, storage, handling, cooking, etc. — keep food safe, prevent contamination. Follow good hygiene practices. |
| **Food Safety Act 1990 / Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013** | Legal framework for food safety — compliance with food safety requirements, due diligence, etc. Similar regulations in Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland. |
| **Food Standards Agency (FSA)** | UK food safety regulator — guidance, standards, food hygiene guidance. |
| **Food hygiene rating scheme (FHRS)** | Local authorities inspect food businesses and give a food hygiene rating (0-5) — displayed (in England, Wales, Northern Ireland — Scotland has its own scheme). Higher rating = better. Important for customer confidence — customers look at hygiene ratings. |
| **Food safety management** | Have a food safety management system — based on HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) principles — record keeping, procedures, temperature control, cross-contamination prevention, cleaning, etc. |
| **Allergen information** | Provide allergen information for food/drink — 14 major allergens (celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphur dioxide/sulphites). Menu labelling, allergen information provided to customers, allergen matrix, etc. |
| **Staff training** | Food hygiene training for food handling staff — level 2 food hygiene certificate (or equivalent) commonly required/recommended for food handlers. Some roles may need higher level training (level 3 for managers). |
| **Premises hygiene** | Clean premises, adequate facilities (sinks, washing, storage, refrigeration, cooking equipment, etc.), pest control, handwashing facilities, etc. |
| **Food business licence / registration for specific activities** | Some specific food activities may require specific registrations, licences, approvals (e.g., meat products, dairy, etc. — regulated food establishments — but most restaurants/cafes don't need this). Check if the food business involves regulated activities. |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD, as a food business (restaurant, cafe, bar with food, pub with food, food truck, catering, cloud kitchen, food manufacturing, etc.), must register with the local authority, comply with food hygiene regulations, have a food safety management system, provide allergen information, and undergo hygiene inspections (food hygiene rating). Staff handling food should have food hygiene training. This is essential for food businesses.

## Step 3: Alcohol Licensing — Premises Licence and Personal Licence

If the food/beverage business serves alcohol — bar, pub, restaurant with alcohol, cafe with alcohol, etc. — alcohol licensing is required.

| Licence | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Premises licence** | Licence for the premises authorising the sale of alcohol, supply of late-night refreshment, regulated entertainment (e.g., live music, performance) — granted by the local authority (council). Required for any premises selling alcohol. |
| **Personal licence** | Personal licence held by an individual authorising them to authorise sale of alcohol on premises licensed for alcohol sales — granted by local authority. At least one personal licence holder needed. Applicant must be fit and proper person. |

**Key points:**

- **Premises licence** — applied for by the company (UK LTD can apply, with a designated premises supervisor who holds a personal licence). Sets out licensable activities, opening hours, conditions.
- **Personal licence** — held by an individual — designated premises supervisor (DPS) for alcohol sales should hold a personal licence.
- **Licensing objectives** — prevention of crime and disorder, public safety, prevention of public nuisance, protection of children from harm.
- **Licensing conditions** — premises licence may have conditions — opening hours, security, noise, etc.
- **Licensing authority** — local council handles applications and hearings.

**For the UK LTD:**

- UK LTD can apply for premises licence (as the company).
- An individual (director, manager, or other) with a personal licence should be the DPS for alcohol sales.
- If UK LTD owns the pub/restaurant/bar, the company applies for premises licence; a personal licence holder is the DPS.

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

If you are a non-resident entrepreneur running a UK bar, pub, restaurant with alcohol, the UK LTD applies for the premises licence. You (or a trusted individual in the UK) need a personal licence as DPS. If you are abroad, you may need a UK-based individual to hold the personal licence and act as DPS. Important — alcohol licensing requires a personal licence holder in the UK.

## Step 4: VAT on Food and Drink — Zero-Rated Food, Standard-Rated Meals, Alcohol

VAT on food and drink is complex — different food and drink items have different VAT rates.

**VAT on food and drink:**

| Supply | VAT treatment | Notes |
|--------|---------------|-------|
| **Food ingredients (basic food items)** | Generally zero-rated for VAT (0%) | Basic food items — e.g., bread, milk, vegetables, fruit, meat, fish, eggs, cheese, cereals, etc. — generally zero-rated. (Specific rules — not all food is zero-rated; some food is standard-rated even if basic.) |
| **Prepared food (restaurant meals, cafe meals, etc.)** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Prepared food — restaurant meals, cafe food, prepared dishes — generally standard-rated. |
| **Hot takeaway food** | Standard-rated for VAT (20%) in most cases | Hot takeaway food — generally standard-rated (with some exceptions — e.g., certain hot takeaway food may be zero-rated in specific circumstances, but most hot takeaway is standard-rated). |
| **Cold takeaway food** | Zero-rated (0%) in some cases | Certain cold takeaway food may be zero-rated (specific conditions — e.g., sandwiches, cold snacks?). Check specific items. |
| **Cakes, biscuits, confectionery, etc.** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Cakes, biscuits, confectionery, crisps, etc. — generally standard-rated (these are standard-rated "luxury" food items). |
| **Ice cream, sweets, etc.** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Ice cream, sweets — standard-rated. |
| **Alcohol** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Alcohol — standard-rated (beer, wine, spirits, etc.). (Alcohol duty is separate — excise duty on alcohol.) |
| **Coffee, tea, etc. (prepared hot drinks)** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Prepared hot drinks (coffee, tea, hot chocolate, etc. sold in a cafe/restaurant) — generally standard-rated. (Coffee beans are zero-rated? Coffee as a prepared drink is standard-rated.) |
| **Cold drinks (non-alcoholic)** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Cold drinks (soft drinks, etc.) sold in a cafe/restaurant — standard-rated. (Some bottled water may be zero-rated? Check.) |
| **Restaurant service / service charge** | Standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Service charge — standard-rated (if it's a separate charge for service). |
| **Catering** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) | Catering services — standard-rated. |
| **Food sold in a bakery shop (e.g., bread, cakes)** | Some zero-rated, some standard-rated depending on the item | Bread — certain bread is zero-rated. Cakes, pastries — standard-rated (generally). Complex — depends on the specific product. |
| **Food sold in a market stall** | Depends on the food — some zero-rated, some standard-rated | Market food — depends on the items. |
| **Food delivery (online food delivery)** | Depends on the supply — food (zero-rated ingredients vs standard-rated prepared food), delivery charges | Food delivery — the food supply VAT (as above), and delivery charges (may be standard-rated or zero-rated depending on the circumstances — e.g., delivery of zero-rated food may be zero-rated? Complex. Generally, delivery charged separately may be standard-rated or zero-rated depending on the main supply). |

**VAT registration:**

- **Mandatory** — over £90,000 rolling 12-month turnover — must register.
- **Voluntary** — below threshold, can register voluntarily — reclaim VAT on business costs (food supplies (if standard-rated VAT on supplies), kitchen equipment, premises costs, alcohol stock, marketing, professional fees, etc.) and appear more established. However, if mostly selling zero-rated food, voluntary registration may not be beneficial (can't reclaim much input VAT if supplies are zero-rated). Need to consider.

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD, if VAT-registered, handles VAT on food and drink correctly — zero-rated basic food ingredients, standard-rated prepared food, alcohol (standard-rated), catering, etc. For online food sales, different rules may apply. Multi-currency banking (Wise/Revolut) for international suppliers and customers.

## Step 5: Premises — Restaurant, Cafe, Bar, Pub, Food Truck, Cloud Kitchen

Food and beverage businesses need premises — restaurant, cafe, bar, pub, food truck pitch, cloud kitchen, brewery, etc. — and premises considerations are significant.

**Premises considerations:**

| Consideration | Notes |
|---------------|-------|
| **Type of premises** | Restaurant unit, cafe unit, bar/pub premises, food truck/trailer, cloud kitchen (industrial/commercial kitchen), brewery/distillery premises, food manufacturing premises, market stall/pitch, etc. |
| **Location** | High street, shopping centre, mall, business park, tourist area, residential area, market, street pitch, industrial area (for cloud kitchen/manufacturing), etc. — depends on target market, footfall, visibility, competition, rent affordability, accessibility. |
| **Planning permission** | Check planning for the business use — change of use may be needed (e.g., office to restaurant, retail to restaurant, etc.), some uses are restricted. Check with local authority planning. Pubs may have specific planning considerations. Food trucks/stalls — street trading permissions, pitch permissions. |
| **Use class** | Restaurants, cafes — may be sui generis or specific use class. Pubs — sui generis. Bars — sui generis. Check appropriate use class. |
| **Lease/purchase** | Commercial lease (or purchase) — lease terms, deposit, rent, business rates, length, break clauses, repair provisions, etc. |
| **Business rates** | Non-domestic property rates (business rates) — applies to commercial food premises (restaurants, cafes, bars, pubs, cloud kitchens, manufacturing, etc.). Check rateable value and reliefs. |
| **Licensing** | Alcohol licence (premises licence) for premises selling alcohol. Late-night refreshment licence (if selling hot food/drink late at night, 11pm-5am, in some areas). Street trading licence for food truck/stall pitches (local authority). Any other licences/permissions. |
| **Building/fire safety** | Premises must meet building regulations, fire safety, licensing conditions, health and safety. Fire risk assessment. |
| **Kitchen and equipment** | Kitchen equipment, cooking equipment, ventilation, extraction (especially important for restaurants/cafes — extract systems, grease traps, etc.), refrigeration, washing facilities, etc. Proper kitchen facilities required for food businesses. |
| **Food hygiene facilities** | Sinks, washing, storage, refrigeration, handwashing, etc. — adequate facilities for food hygiene. |

## Step 8: Food and Beverage Regulations — Labelling, Alcohol Duty, Excise

Food and beverage businesses must comply with regulations — food labelling, alcohol duty/excise (if producing alcohol), food safety, etc.

**Key regulations:**

| Area | Details |
|------|---------|

| **Food standards** | Food standards — food safety, food quality, food labelling, additives, etc. FSA guidance. |
| **Weights and measures** | If selling food by weight/measure — weights and measures regulations, accurate weighing, labelling of weight. |
| **Health and safety** | Health and safety at work — kitchen safety, knife safety, hot surfaces, slips/trips, manual handling, fire safety (cooking equipment, extraction systems), gas safety (if using gas), electrical safety, etc. |
| **Noise (if relevant)** | If bar/pub with late-night service — noise considerations, licensing conditions, noise management. |
| **Entertainment licensing (if applicable)** | If providing regulated entertainment (live music, performance, etc.) — may need to be specified in premises licence (or may be exempt under certain conditions — e.g., live music exemptions under Licensing Act 2003, s.101?). |
| **Street trading (if food truck/stall)** | Street trading licence/permissions from local authority for street food pitches, market stalls, etc. |
| **Environmental permits (if producing alcohol — brewery/distillery)** | Brewery/distillery — may need environmental permit for production (wastewater, emissions, etc.) — check environmental regulations. |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD must comply with relevant food and beverage regulations — food labelling (allergen information), alcohol duty/excise (if producing alcohol — brewery/distillery — critical), alcohol licensing (if selling alcohol), food safety, health and safety, street trading (if applicable), environmental permits (if production).
