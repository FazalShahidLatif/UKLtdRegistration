## Step 2: Intellectual Property for Franchise & Licensing Businesses

Intellectual property is central to franchise and licensing businesses. The UK LTD should own the IP — trademarks, brand, know-how, systems, manuals, software, etc. — and license it to franchisees and licensees.

**Types of IP in franchise/licensing:**

| IP type | Description | Relevance to franchise/licensing |
|---------|-------------|----------------------------------|
| **Trademarks** | Brand names, logos, slogans, trade dress — the brand identity | Core to franchise and brand licensing — the brand is what franchisees/licensees license |
| **Copyright** | Written materials, manuals, training materials, marketing materials, software, website content, etc. | Operations manuals, training materials, marketing materials, software used in the franchise/licensing system |
| **Patents** | Inventions, technical solutions (if applicable) | If the franchise/licensing business involves patented technology or processes |
| **Trade secrets / know-how** | Confidential business information, systems, processes, recipes, methods, customer lists, etc. | Core to many franchises — the know-how and systems that make the business work |
| **Designs** | Product designs, packaging, visual appearance | If the franchise/licensing involves designed products or packaging |
| **Domain names** | Website addresses | Brand website, franchise portal, etc. |
| **Database rights** | Customer lists, supplier lists, etc. (where applicable) | Customer databases, etc. |

**IP ownership structure:**

- The UK LTD should own the IP — trademarks, copyright in manuals and materials, know-how, etc.
- The IP is then licensed to franchisees/licensees under the franchise/licensing agreement.
- Clear IP ownership is essential — the franchisor/licensor must own or control the IP it licenses.

**Trademark registration:**

- Register trademarks in the UK via UK IPO (UK Intellectual Property Office).
- For international protection, consider the Madrid Protocol — international trademark registration through UK IPO covering multiple countries.
- Trademark registration gives exclusive rights to the brand in the registered territories.

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

If you are a non-resident entrepreneur with a brand, business concept, or IP you want to franchise or license globally, a UK LTD can own the IP, register trademarks (UK and internationally), and license the IP to franchisees and licensees worldwide — receiving franchise fees and royalties in multiple currencies through your UK LTD's multi-currency account.

## Step 3: How Franchising Works — Business Format Franchising

Business format franchising is the most common form — the franchisor licenses the entire business format: brand, products/services, operating systems, know-how, training, ongoing support, marketing, etc.

**Key elements of a franchise system:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Franchise brand** | The brand — name, logo, trade dress, identity — that the franchisee operates under |
| **Products/services** | The products or services the franchisee offers — under the brand, according to the franchisor's system |
| **Operating system / know-how** | The way the business operates — systems, processes, procedures, standards — the know-how the franchisor provides |
| **Operations manual** | The detailed manual covering how the franchise operates — procedures, standards, requirements, etc. |
| **Training** | Initial and ongoing training for franchisees and their staff — how to operate the franchise |
| **Support** | Ongoing support from the franchisor — operational support, marketing support, advisory, etc. |
| **Marketing / advertising fund** | Contributions to a collective marketing/advertising fund (often a % of turnover) for brand marketing |
| **Quality standards** | Standards the franchisee must meet — quality, service, appearance, etc. |
| **Franchisor controls** | Controls the franchisor exercises over the franchisee — to protect the brand and system |
| **Franchise fees and royalties** | Initial franchise fee + ongoing royalties (typically % of turnover or fixed fee) + marketing fund contributions |

**Types of franchise arrangements:**

| Arrangement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Single-unit franchise** | One franchise unit — one territory/location |
| **Multi-unit franchise** | Multiple franchise units — area development agreement for multiple units in a territory |
| **Master franchise** | The master franchisee has the right to develop the franchise in a territory and can sub-franchise (sell franchises) within that territory — acts as franchisor in that territory |
| **Area development agreement** | Rights to develop multiple units in a territory within a timeframe |
| **Conversion franchise** | Existing business converts to become a franchise unit of the franchisor's system |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

A UK LTD can be a franchisor with international franchisees — licensing the brand and system globally, receiving franchise fees and royalties from franchisees in various countries and currencies, via the UK LTD's multi-currency account. Or the UK LTD can be a franchisee of a UK or international franchise — operating a franchise unit under the franchisor's brand.

## Step 4: How Licensing Works — IP Licensing and Brand Licensing

Licensing is broader than franchising — licensing intellectual property (trademarks, patents, copyrights, know-how) or brand rights to others for a fee/royalty, without necessarily providing the full business format support that franchising involves.

**Key elements of a licensing arrangement:**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Licensed IP/brand** | The IP or brand being licensed — trademarks, patents, copyrights, know-how, brand |
| **Licensee** | The party receiving the license — using the IP/brand under the license |
| **Licensor** | The IP/brand owner granting the license — the UK LTD in this case |
| **License agreement** | The agreement setting out the terms of the license |
| **Scope of license** | What is licensed — which IP, which territories, which products/services, exclusivity |
| **Territory** | Geographic scope — worldwide, specific countries, regions, exclusive/non-exclusive territories |
| **Exclusivity** | Exclusive license (only this licensee in the territory) vs non-exclusive (licensor can license others) |
| **Royalty structure** | How the licensee pays — royalty % of sales, fixed fee, minimum guarantee + royalty, etc. |
| **Term** | Length of the license — fixed term, renewable, perpetual (less common) |
| **Quality control** | Licensor's right to control quality — especially important for brand licensing to protect the brand |
| **IP ownership** | License does not transfer ownership — licensee gets a right to use, but licensor retains ownership |

**Types of licensing:**

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Trademark license** | License to use trademarks — brand licensing |
| **Patent license** | License to use patented technology |
| **Copyright license** | License to use copyrighted materials |
| **Know-how license** | License to use confidential know-how/technical information |
| **Brand licensing** | License to use brand on products, in marketing, etc. — common in fashion, entertainment, character licensing |
| **Software license** | License to use software — SaaS, on-premise software, etc. |
| **Exclusive license** | Only the licensee can use the IP in the territory — licensor cannot license others |
| **Non-exclusive license** | Licensor can license multiple parties in the same territory |

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

A UK LTD can own IP (trademarks, know-how, software, brand) and license it globally — licensing to companies in various countries, receiving royalties in multiple currencies through the UK LTD's multi-currency account. Or the UK LTD can be a licensee — licensing IP/brand from others to use in its business.

## Step 5: Franchise Agreement Essentials

The franchise agreement is the core contract between franchisor and franchisee. It sets out the rights and obligations of both parties.

**Key terms in a franchise agreement:**

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Grant of franchise** | What is granted — the right to operate a franchise unit under the brand and system, in a territory |
| **Term** | Length of the franchise agreement — e.g., 5 years, 10 years, with renewal options |
| **Territory** | Exclusive or non-exclusive territory for the franchisee |
| **Initial franchise fee** | Upfront fee paid by franchisee to franchisor for the franchise rights, training, setup support |
| **Ongoing royalties** | Ongoing fees — typically a % of franchisee's turnover/revenue, paid regularly (monthly/quarterly) |
| **Marketing/advertising contributions** | Contributions to national/regional marketing fund — often a % of turnover |
| **Brand and IP license** | License to use the brand, trademarks, IP, systems — subject to terms and conditions |
| **Operations manual** | Requirement to follow the operations manual and system standards |
| **Training** | Initial training requirement, ongoing training requirements |
| **Support** | Support the franchisor provides — operational, marketing, advisory |
| **Quality standards** | Standards the franchisee must maintain — quality, service, appearance, etc. |
| **Franchisor controls/inspections** | Franchisor's right to inspect, audit, enforce standards |
| **Restrictions** | Restrictions on franchisee — e.g., can't operate other businesses, must purchase from approved suppliers, etc. |
| **Franchisor obligations** | What the franchisor must do — provide the system, training, support, marketing, etc. |
| **Franchisee obligations** | What the franchisee must do — pay fees, operate according to system, maintain standards, etc. |
| **Termination** | How the agreement can be terminated — by either party, for breach, for convenience (if allowed), notice periods |
| **Renewal** | Options to renew the agreement at the end of the term |
| **Post-termination** | Obligations after termination — cease use of brand/IP, hand over materials, etc. |
| **Dispute resolution** | How disputes are resolved — negotiation, mediation, arbitration, court |
| **Governing law and jurisdiction** | Which law applies and which courts have jurisdiction |

**For franchisees — what to look for:**

- Franchisor's reputation and track record
- Franchise disclosure — information about the franchise, financial performance (where available), existing franchisees
- Total investment required — initial fee, setup costs, working capital
- Ongoing fees and royalties — and what they cover
- Territory and exclusivity
- Training and support provided
- Restrictions and obligations
- Term and renewal
- Exit strategy — resale, termination, post-termination
- Legal review of the franchise agreement by a lawyer experienced in franchising

**For non-resident franchisees:**

If you are a non-resident entrepreneur looking to buy a franchise, a UK LTD can be the franchisee entity — entering into the franchise agreement, paying the franchise fee and royalties (in GBP or other currencies via multi-currency account), operating the franchise. The UK LTD gives a professional entity for the franchise relationship.

## Step 6: VAT on Franchise & Licensing Income

VAT is a key consideration for franchise and licensing businesses. The VAT treatment depends on the type of income and who it's paid by.

**VAT on franchise and licensing income:**

| Income type | VAT treatment (UK VAT) |
|-------------|------------------------|
| **Initial franchise fee** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) if the franchisor is VAT-registered and the fee is for the grant of the franchise (a taxable supply of services). |
| **Ongoing royalties** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) — royalties for the continued use of the brand/IP/system are taxable supplies. |
| **Marketing fund contributions** | Depends — if the contribution is for the franchisee's own marketing, may be outside VAT; if the franchisor collects and spends on behalf of franchisees, may be outside VAT or standard-rated depending on arrangement. |
| **Franchise consulting services** | Standard-rated for VAT (20%) — consulting services are taxable supplies. |
| **Licensing fees (IP/brand licensing)** | Generally standard-rated for VAT (20%) — licensing of IP is a taxable supply of services. |
| **Sale of goods through franchise** | Normal VAT on goods — if the franchisee sells goods, VAT on those sales (if VAT-registered). |
| **Training services** | Standard-rated for VAT (20%) — training services are taxable supplies. |

**International VAT considerations:**

| Scenario | VAT treatment |
|----------|----------------|
| **Franchise fees from overseas franchisees** | Place of supply of services to a business (B2B) — generally where the customer (franchisee) belongs — so may be outside UK VAT (reverse charge in franchisee's country). If B2C (individual franchisee), may be UK VAT. Depends on circumstances. |
| **Royalties from overseas licensees** | Place of supply of royalty services — generally where the customer (licensee) belongs — may be outside UK VAT (reverse charge). |
| **Licensing IP to overseas licensees** | Generally outside UK VAT for B2B — reverse charge in licensee's country. |
| **Franchise/licensing income from EU customers** | Post-Brexit — place of supply rules apply — for B2B services, generally where the customer belongs (outside UK VAT). For B2C, may be UK VAT or may require EU VAT registration depending on the service. |

**VAT registration for franchise/licensing businesses:**

- **Mandatory threshold** — currently £90,000 rolling 12-month turnover — must register if over.
- **Voluntary registration** — below threshold, can register voluntarily — reclaim VAT on business costs (legal fees, marketing, IP registration, travel, etc.), and if selling to VAT-registered businesses (e.g., franchise fees from VAT-registered franchisees), the reverse charge applies and you can reclaim input VAT.
- **International situations** — if making supplies subject to EU VAT (e.g., digital services to EU consumers, certain franchise/licensing supplies), may need EU VAT registration/OSS.

**For non-resident entrepreneurs:**

Your UK LTD, if VAT-registered, handles VAT on franchise fees, royalties, licensing income, and consulting services per the rules above. For international franchise/licensing income, the place of supply rules determine UK VAT treatment — generally B2B supplies to overseas customers are outside UK VAT (reverse charge in customer's country). Multi-currency banking (Wise/Revolut) handles receiving franchise fees and royalties in various currencies.

## Next Steps for Franchise & Licensing

1. **Form your UK LTD** — choose your package at [/pricing](/pricing)
2. **Develop your franchise system or IP/brand** — the franchise package, IP, brand, operations manual, training, support systems
3. **Protect your IP** — trademark registration (UK IPO, Madrid for international), copyright, know-how protection, confidentiality
4. **Ensure IP ownership in the UK LTD** — assign IP to the company, so the company owns what it licenses
5. **Set up Wise Business or Revolut Business** for multi-currency banking — essential for international franchise fees, royalties, licensing income
6. **VAT registration** — if needed (over threshold or voluntary) — for franchise fees, royalties, licensing income, consulting
7. **Prepare franchise/licensing agreements** — with a lawyer experienced in franchising/licensing — franchise agreement, licensing agreement, etc.
8. **Develop your franchise/licensing marketing** — franchise opportunity marketing, brand marketing, sales materials
9. **Recruit franchisees/licensees** — franchise sales, licensee negotiations, territory planning
