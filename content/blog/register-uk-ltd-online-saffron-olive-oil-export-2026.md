# Saffron & Olive Oil Export: How a UK LTD Bridges EU Producers to Arab World Buyers

If you are in the business of moving premium food products between Europe and the Arab world, a UK LTD gives you something that no other setup can match: a single registered company that handles EU sourcing, UK customs clearance, multi-currency payments, and Arab market contracts — all under one legal roof.

This guide covers exactly how that works for two of the highest-value food export categories: saffron and olive oil.

## Why Saffron and Olive Oil?

Both are high-value, low-volume products where the margin justifies the paperwork. A kilogram of premium Spanish saffron can wholesale for £800-£2,000. A tin of certified extra virgin olive oil from Tuscany or Kalamata commands a premium in Gulf supermarkets. The buyers — luxury retailers, high-end restaurants, spice merchants, gift wholesalers in Dubai, Riyadh, Doha, Kuwait City — are willing to pay for quality and traceability.

The challenge is that the supply chain crosses three jurisdictions: EU producer → UK hub → Arab buyer. Each border crossing needs documentation. Each currency conversion eats margin if you are not set up correctly. A UK LTD solves all of that.

## Step 2: Get Your EORI Number

An EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number is required for any UK business that imports or exports goods. Without it, your saffron and olive oil cannot clear UK customs.

**What you need:**

- Your UK LTD company number (from Companies House)
- Your registered office address
- A contact person

**How to apply:** Apply online via the UK government's EORI service. It is free. Most applications are approved within 24-48 hours.

Once you have your EORI, you can:

- Import saffron and olive oil from EU member states into UK customs
- Export saffron and olive oil from UK to Arab world destinations
- File customs declarations for both inward and outward movements

## Step 3: Import from EU — Saffron and Olive Oil Sourcing

### Sourcing Saffron from Spain

Spain produces roughly 75% of Europe's saffron, much of it from La Mancha (Castilla-La Mancha region, around Ávila, Toledo, Madrid). The D.O.P. (Denominación de Origen Protegida) "Azafrán de La Mancha" is the gold standard — it is certified, traceable, and commands premium prices.

**What to look for:**

| Grade | ISO 3632 Category | Typical use | Price indicator |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Category I | Highest (crocin, picrocrocin, safranal all top-tier) | Luxury retail, high-end restaurants, gifting | Highest |
| Category II | Good quality | Standard retail, food service | Mid |
| Category III | Lower quality | Blending, lower-end products | Low |

For Arab market exports, aim for **Category I or II**. Gulf buyers know ISO 3632 grading and expect it on the certificate.

**Spanish saffron cooperatives to approach:**

- Cooperatives in La Mancha region (Castilla-La Mancha)
- Producers in Navarre and Aragon (also significant saffron regions)
- Look for D.O.P. certification as a quality signal

### Sourcing Olive Oil from Italy, Greece, and Spain

The major olive oil producing regions in the EU:

| Country | Key regions | Notable styles |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Italy | Tuscany, Puglia, Sicily, Calabria, Umbria | Extra virgin, DOP certified, single-variety |
| Greece | Kalamata, Crete, Lesvos, Peloponnese | Koroneiki variety, robust, high polyphenol |
| Spain | Andalucía (Jaén, Córdoba, Granada), Catalonia | Picual, Arbequina, Hojiblanca varieties |

**Quality signals Arab buyers look for:**

- **DOP/IGP certification** (Protected Designation of Origin / Protected Geographical Indication)
- **Extra virgin** grade (acidity below 0.8%)
- **Harvest year** clearly marked
- **Single-variety** or single-region where possible (commands premium)
- **Dark glass or tin packaging** (protects quality during shipping)

**EU suppliers to build relationships with:**

- Italian cooperatives and consortiums (Consorzio di Tutela dell'Olio di Oliva Italiano)
- Greek olive oil producers' associations
- Spanish D.O.P. saffron and olive oil regulatory councils

The key is to establish **long-term purchase agreements** rather than one-off buys. Arab buyers value consistency — they want the same quality grade every order. Your UK LTD becomes the contracting entity with these EU suppliers, which gives you leverage on price and consistency.

## Step 4: UK Customs and VAT — What Happens at the Border

### Importing from EU to UK

Since Brexit, goods moving from EU to UK are subject to UK customs procedures. Here is what happens when your UK LTD imports saffron or olive oil from Spain/Italy/Greece:

1. **Customs declaration** — Your EORI number is used to file an import declaration
2. **Commodity code** — Saffron and olive oil each have specific HS codes:
   - Saffron: **0908.20** (Saffron, dried)
   - Olive oil: **1509.10** (Olive oil, virgin) or **1509.90** (Other olive oil)
3. **VAT** — Imports from EU to UK are subject to UK import VAT (standard rate 20%) at the border. You can recover this through your UK VAT return if registered.
4. **Duties** — Check the UK Trade Tariff for current duty rates on these commodity codes. Some may be duty-free or low-duty under trade agreements.

### Exporting from UK to Arab World

When your UK LTD exports saffron or olive oil to UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, Egypt, or Morocco:

1. **Export declaration** — Filed using your EORI
2. **Commercial invoice** — Must include: seller (your UK LTD), buyer (Arab company), description, quantity, value,Incoterms (e.g., FOB London, CIF Dubai)
3. **VAT** — Exports outside the UK are **zero-rated** for VAT purposes. You do not charge UK VAT on exports to Arab countries. You must keep evidence of export (customs declaration, transport documents).
4. **Certificate of Origin** — May be required by Arab buyers or their customs authorities. Issued by chambers of commerce in UK. Confirms goods originated in UK (or where the goods were produced — clarify with buyer).
5. **Phytosanitary certificates** — May be required for plant-based products (saffron, olive oil) depending on destination country requirements. Check with the Arab buyer's customs authority.

**Important:** Each Arab country has its own import requirements. Always confirm with your buyer what documentation they need before shipping.

## Step 7: Quality Grading and Certification — Your Competitive Edge

Arab buyers pay premiums for **verified quality**. Your UK LTD can add value by:

### Saffron Grading (ISO 3632)

ISO 3632 defines three chemical parameters for saffron quality:

| Parameter | What it measures | Higher = better |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Crocin** | Colour intensity | More crocin = richer colour |
| **Picrocrocin** | Taste/bitterness | More picrocrocin = stronger flavour |
| **Safranal** | Aroma | More safranal = stronger aroma |

Category I (highest) requires crocin ≥ 100, picrocrocin ≥ 100, safranal ≥ 50 (in specific units). A UK LTD that imports bulk saffron, gets it graded at a UK lab to ISO 3632, and presents the certificate to Arab buyers — that is a premium product.

### Olive Oil Certification

| Certification | What it means | Value for Arab buyers |
|---------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Extra Virgin** | Acidity < 0.8%, no sensory defects | Table standard for premium market |
| **DOP/IGP** | Protected origin, verified production zone | Traceability, authenticity |
| **Single variety** | 100% one olive cultivar | Premium positioning, distinctive flavour |
| **Early harvest** | Olives picked green, higher polyphenols | Health positioning, premium price |
| **Organic** | Certified organic production | Health-conscious buyers, higher price |

A UK LTD warehouse can hold graded, certified product and present it to Arab buyers with the full documentation package. This is what turns a commodity buyer into a premium buyer.

## Step 10: Afghan and Pakistani Saffron — An Ethical Sourcing Opportunity

This is a special angle within the saffron export business that connects to social impact.

**Afghanistan** (particularly Kandahar region) produces some of the world's highest-quality saffron. Afghan saffron is often rated Category I under ISO 3632. But Afghan saffron farmers are among the poorest agricultural workers in the world — they lack direct access to premium international markets, and middlemen often buy their saffron at low prices.

**Pakistan** (Hazara region, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) also produces saffron on a smaller scale. Same dynamic — small-scale farmers with limited market access.

**How a UK LTD changes this:**

A UK LTD can:

1. **Import Afghan/Pakistani saffron directly** — under UK customs, with proper documentation
2. **Grade it to ISO 3632 in the UK** — present certified quality to Arab buyers
3. **Export to Gulf buyers at premium prices** — UAE, Saudi, Qatar markets pay premium for certified, traceable saffron
4. **Create a fair-trade story** — "This saffron supports Afghan/Pakistani farming communities" is a powerful narrative for Arab luxury buyers who care about provenance and ethics

The margin uplift from grading + certification + direct Gulf access can be reinvested into buying better prices from the farmers — creating a cycle where the UK LTD adds value at each step and shares it back.

**Content angle:** "UK LTD for Afghan and Pakistani Saffron Farmers: Access Gulf Premium Markets" — this is the kind of story that gets media coverage, builds trust, and differentiates your brand from generic food exporters.
